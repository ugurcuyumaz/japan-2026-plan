# Tokyo-Based Excursions & Day Trips (31 Oct - 11 Nov 2026)

Context: 2nd Japan trip for a Spanish couple based in Tokyo for 11 nights. Trip 1 covered
Tokyo (4d), Kyoto, Nara, Osaka, Hiroshima, Kobe, Himeji. Nikko is excluded here (researched
separately). Hard constraints: no rental car, no serious hiking (sightseeing walks only),
both have tattoos (private/rentable baths only), foliage not a ranking priority (flag actual
closures only), they like food and novelty.

---

## 1. Hakone

**What's there:** The classic "Hakone Loop" — Odakyu Romancecar to Hakone-Yumoto, Hakone
Tozan switchback railway to Gora, cablecar to Sounzan, Hakone Ropeway over Owakudani to
Togendai, then the Lake Ashi "pirate ship" cruise back toward Hakone-machi/Moto-Hakone, plus
the Hakone Open-Air Museum (sculpture park) and the Pola Museum of Art.

**Volcanic status (IMPORTANT, checked live):** Owakudani has a real history of closures —
the ropeway/valley station was shut for safety from 2015 gas-vent activity and has reopened
and re-closed multiple times since. As of the most recent info, the **Owakudani Ropeway
station was rebuilt and reopened April 25, 2025** with a new "Earth Valley" observation deck
([hakonenavi.jp](https://www.hakonenavi.jp/international/en/article/4226),
[timeout.com](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/news/hakone-ropeway-to-open-new-observation-area-overlooking-a-volcanic-valley-041625)).
However, operators explicitly warn conditions can change with no notice —
"gondola operation is suspended when volcanic activity increases to an unsafe level... check
the website in advance" ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/national-parks/parks/fuji-hakone-izu/see-and-do/hakone-ropeway/)),
and the historic ~1km walking trail near the vents has been closed since 2015 and remains so
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cwakudani)). **Action for Nov 2026: check
Hakone Ropeway's live status page a few days before the visit; have a backup (skip Owakudani,
do the rest of the loop) in mind.**

**Hakone Freepass:** Issued by Odakyu. Following October 2025 fare revisions, current
2026 adult price from Shinjuku is **¥7,100 for 2 days / ¥7,500 for 3 days**, covering round
trip Shinjuku–Odawara plus unlimited use of 8 local transport modes (Tozan train, cablecar,
ropeway, pirate ship, Hakone Tozan buses) and discounts at 50+ attractions
([japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/hakone-free-pass),
[navitime.com](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/guide/NTJjtg2602009-en/)). It saves
roughly ¥4,500–5,500 vs buying each leg separately. Romancecar seat reservation is a small
add-on (~¥1,150–1,200 one way) if you want the limited express rather than local trains
([japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2358_014.html)). The pass has **no 1-day
version** — minimum is 2 consecutive days, which nudges Hakone toward an overnight rather than
a rushed day trip if you want to do the full loop plus a museum.

**Private-bath ryokan reachable car-free:** Hakone is full of car-free-accessible ryokan with
private in-room open-air baths (common regional style). Representative options found in
searches include properties in Hakone-Yumoto and Gora reachable by the Tozan railway/bus
network — no vehicle needed, matching the tattoo constraint via private baths rather than
communal ones.

**Verdict:** Doing the full loop (both museums + ropeway + cruise) in a single day from Tokyo
is tight and rushed. Given the Freepass is priced for 2-3 days anyway, **1 night in
Hakone-Yumoto or Gora is the better use of it** — arrive late morning, do half the loop +
Open-Air Museum day 1, private onsen dinner/evening, finish the loop + Pola Museum day 2 on
the way back. As a pure day trip it's doable but feels compressed, especially with museum time.

## 2. Fuji Five Lakes / Kawaguchiko

**Access:** The **Fuji Excursion** limited express runs direct from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko,
no transfer, in about **1h50–1h55min**, fare around **¥4,130** one way (basic fare ¥2,390 +
express fee ¥1,620, per Fujikyu's own site: http://e.fujikyu-railway.jp/fujikaiyuu/). Confirmed
across multiple sources: [matcha-jp.com](https://matcha-jp.com/en/19798),
[jrailpass.com](https://www.jrailpass.com/blog/fuji-excursion-train),
[gowithguide.com](https://gowithguide.com/blog/how-to-book-fuji-excursion-4138) (¥4,130 one way
as of Nov 2025). Only 4 trains/day each direction, so it needs a bit of planning around
timetables. Highway buses are cheaper (¥2,000–3,000) but take 2–2.5h
([itimaker.com](https://www.itimaker.com/blog/tokyo-to-mt-fuji-travel-time)).

**Fuji visibility odds in early November:** Not separately quantified in sources checked, but
November is generally considered one of the better months for Fuji visibility (clear autumn
air, before winter storm systems) — no guarantee, but better odds than summer haze.

**Chureito Pagoda — FLAG: this is a real climb.** Multiple independent sources confirm
**~398–400 stone steps** up from Arakura Sengen Shrine to the pagoda/viewing platform
([japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e6917.html),
[allabout-japan.com](https://allabout-japan.com/en/article/1299/),
[jrpass.com](https://www.jrpass.com/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-chureito-pagoda),
[alltrails.com](https://www.alltrails.com/en-gb/parks/japan/yamanashi/arakurayama-sengen-park/short)
calls it "a steep climb of nearly 400 stone steps"). One source
([tokyoweekender.com](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/location/chureito-pagoda/)) notes "an
alternative gentler slope zigzags up the hillside for those who prefer to avoid stairs" — a
usable workaround, but it's still uphill walking, not flat. **This borders on the
"no serious hiking" line — it's a staircase climb, not a hike, and can be done at a slow pace
with rest stops, but it is real vertical effort and should be flagged to the couple as
optional/skippable if they'd rather not.**

**Momiji Corridor (Fujikawaguchiko Autumn Leaves Festival):** Multiple 2025 sources confirm
the festival runs from **early November through the end of November** (specifically
Nov 1–30, 2025 per [stripes.com](https://japan.stripes.com/travel/fuji-kawaguchiko-fall-leaves-festival.html)
and [kawaguchiko.net](https://en.kawaguchiko.net/event-en/)) — so **the couple's 31 Oct–11 Nov
window falls squarely inside the 2026 edition of this festival** (assuming similar dates
repeat, which is typical for this annual event). ~400–500 maple trees line the lakeshore with
night illumination. This is a genuine, currently-running attraction for their exact dates —
worth calling out even though foliage isn't a priority, since it's "there anyway."

**Other spots:** Oishi Park (lavender in season, less relevant Nov; still a flat lakeside walk
with Fuji views), Kachi Kachi Yama ropeway (short, easy, no hike), Fuji-Q Highland (thrill-ride
park — novelty factor but skews younger/thrill-seeker; can be skipped or done briefly).

**Private onsen:** Kawaguchiko has several high-end ryokan with **private in-room open-air
baths with Fuji views** — e.g. Fufu Kawaguchiko, Kaze no Terrace Kukuna, Shuhoukaku Kogetsu,
Yamagishi Ryokan, Mizno Hotel, Hotel Konansou (sources: japanese-onsen.com listings, gltjp.com,
walkintoluxury.com). This is one of the strongest private-onsen options within day-trip range
of Tokyo.

**Verdict:** Comfortably a **day trip** (round-trip travel ~4h on the Fuji Excursion alone,
so plan a full day), OR a very pleasant **1-night stay** if they want a relaxed private-onsen
evening with a Fuji view rather than a rushed there-and-back. Given they already have Hakone
as a stronger overnight-onsen candidate, Kawaguchiko works well as a **day trip**, skipping or
optional-izing the Chureito stair climb.

## 3. Kamakura + Enoshima

**What's there:** Great Buddha (Kotoku-in), Hasedera temple, Tsurugaoka Hachimangu shrine, the
scenic **Enoden** coastal tram line, Enoshima island, and Komachi-dori food street.

**Access & time:** Fastest route is the **JR Yokosuka Line direct from Tokyo Station, ~57 min,
~¥950** ([itimaker.com](https://www.itimaker.com/blog/how-to-get-to-kamakura-from-tokyo)).
Alternative: Odakyu **Enoshima-Kamakura Freepass** from Shinjuku (~¥1,640 round trip) which
also gives unlimited Enoden rides for the day
([navitime.com](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/guide/NTJnews0254-en/),
[japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/ad/enoshima/)). The Enoden itself connects
Kamakura and Enoshima in about 25 minutes
([japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/enoshima-from-kamakura-day-trip)).

**Stairs/climbs to flag:** Enoshima island has a hillside shrine complex and an observation
tower reached via stairs/escalators up the island's slope — not comparable to Chureito's 400
steps, but there is meaningful uphill walking on the island if visiting the upper shrine and
Enoshima Sea Candle tower. Manageable at a relaxed pace; not "serious hiking."

**Verdict:** Excellent, easy, well-trodden **single day trip**. Total round-trip transit is
under 2 hours combined, leaving a full day for sightseeing + food. Good novelty value even
after Kyoto/Nara temples, since the coastal setting (Enoden tram along the sea, Enoshima
island) is a different flavor from the couple's first-trip destinations.

## 4. Yokohama

**What's there:** Minato Mirai waterfront, Cup Noodles Museum, Chinatown (Japan's largest),
Sankeien Garden, Yokohama Air Cabin (urban ropeway).

**Gundam Factory Yokohama — CLOSED, do not include.** Confirmed via multiple sources: it
permanently closed **March 31, 2024** after being extended once from an original 2022 end
date ([timeout.com](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/news/the-yokohama-gundam-factory-has-closed-after-just-three-plus-years-040224),
[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gundam_Factory_Yokohama),
[soranews24.com](https://soranews24.com/2024/04/01/japans-life-size-moving-gundam-closes-down-gets-sent-off-in-style-with-fireworks-drone-art-%E3%80%90video%E3%80%91/amp/)).
The life-size RX-78 Gundam was later reused as a static statue at Expo 2025 in Osaka (which is
also now closed). **There is no Gundam attraction in Yokohama anymore as of 2026 — drop it
from the itinerary entirely.**

**Verdict:** Yokohama is close (Minato Mirai is ~30-40 min from central Tokyo by train) and
pleasant, but without the Gundam draw it's a fairly generic "big-city waterfront + Chinatown"
day, which the couple may find less novel than other options given they've already done big
Japanese cities. **Best treated as a half-day add-on (e.g. paired with Yokosuka, or as a
low-key day near the end of the trip) rather than a dedicated full excursion day.**

## 5. Kawagoe ("Little Edo")

**What's there:** Kurazukuri warehouse street (preserved Edo-Meiji merchant buildings),
Kashiya Yokocho ("Candy Alley"), Toki no Kane bell tower, several temples.

**Access:** Reachable from Ikebukuro/Shinjuku in roughly 30-45 minutes by train (Tobu Tojo
Line or Seibu Shinjuku Line), making it one of the closest options on this list.

**Verdict:** A genuinely relaxed **half-day trip** — flat walking, no stairs/hiking, strong
"old streetscape + snacks" novelty that pairs well as a low-effort morning or afternoon add-on
rather than eating a full day out of the Tokyo block.

## 6. Naruko Onsen / Sendai (1-2 night onsen overnight)

**Access:** Tohoku Shinkansen Tokyo→Sendai (~1h30-1h40), then transfer to a local
line/bus to reach Naruko — total roughly **2.5-3h** each way including the transfer
([japan.travel FR](https://www.japan.travel/fr/destinations/tohoku/miyagi/naruko-area/)
confirms Shinkansen + change at Sendai;
[navitime.com](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/guide/NTJonry2504006-en/) confirms
Shinkansen-to-local-train is the fastest route). Naruko does have its own train station
(Naruko-Onsen Station on the JR Rifle Line).

**Private bath ryokan:** Higashi Naruko Onsen Ryokan Ohnuma has a named private open-air bath
("Mori no Yu", ~10 min walk from the inn) explicitly noted as a highlight
([gltjp.com](https://www.gltjp.com/en/directory/item/16455/)). General private-onsen guides
confirm the region has private/rental options suitable for tattoos
([tinytotintokyo.com](https://tinytotintokyo.com/ryokan-with-private-onsen),
[kashiwaya.org](https://www.kashiwaya.org/e/magazine/onsen/private-onsen-2.html)).

**Matsushima Bay + Sendai gyutan:** Both are easy add-ons en route (Matsushima is a short hop
from Sendai; Sendai itself is famous for grilled beef tongue).

**Honest assessment:** ~5-6 hours of round-trip travel just to reach and return from Naruko is
a substantial chunk of an 11-night Tokyo block, especially when the couple has already done
extensive travel on trip 1 (Hiroshima, Osaka, etc. — they know long transfers). Naruko is a
genuinely lovely, quieter, more "real" onsen town than closer alternatives, but **2 nights
plus ~5-6h of transit total is a stretch relative to what Atami/Izu or even Hakone can deliver
for far less travel time.** It only makes sense if the couple specifically wants a Tohoku/rural
taste and is willing to spend a disproportionate travel/reward ratio for it. Recommend as a
**stretch option, not a top-3 pick**, unless they explicitly want a "far from Tokyo, feels
like a different Japan" experience.

## 7. Atami / Izu (Shuzenji, Jogasaki) — closer onsen alternative

**Access:** Atami is **under 1 hour from Tokyo via the Tokaido Shinkansen**
([rakuten.com](https://travel.rakuten.com/contents/usa/en-us/guide/room-onsen-atami/)) — by
far the fastest onsen-town access on this list. Shuzenji (deeper into Izu) is reached via
Shinkansen to Mishima (~55 min) then a **Izu-Hakone Railway Sunzu Line transfer to Shuzenji
Station (~32 min)** ([haveagood-holiday.com](https://haveagood-holiday.com/en/articles/izu-things-to-do)).

**Private bath ryokan:** Atami has multiple properties with private in-room open-air baths —
e.g. Furuya Ryokan (unique sculpted stone tubs) and others surveyed in
[rakuten.com's roundup](https://travel.rakuten.com/contents/usa/en-us/guide/room-onsen-atami/)
of "Five Must-stay Atami Hotels with Private Hot Spring Baths." Shuzenji has similarly
tattoo-friendly private-bath ryokan such as Yagyu-no-Sho (all 15 rooms have their own onsen,
per [selected-ryokan.com](https://selected-ryokan.com/ryokan/yagyu-no-sho_izu-shuzenji_shizuoka_tokyo-suburbs.html)).

**What to do:** Jogasaki coast is a scenic coastal walking path along volcanic cliffs with a
suspension bridge — walkable, not technical hiking, though there is some uneven terrain and
it can be a full loop of a few km if done in full (shorter viewpoints are accessible with much
less walking). Shuzenji town itself is a compact, walkable onsen town with a bamboo grove path
and its namesake temple. Atami town has a retro seaside-resort character with a good food scene.

**Verdict:** **The strongest onsen-overnight option on this list.** Under an hour each way by
Shinkansen means it barely eats into the Tokyo block — a 1-night stay costs almost no travel
overhead compared to Naruko's multi-hour trek, while still delivering a genuine private-onsen
ryokan experience with sea/mountain scenery. Recommend Atami or Shuzenji over Naruko for time
efficiency, unless the couple specifically wants the "further from Tokyo" Tohoku flavor.

## 8. Nikko-adjacent alternatives: Kusatsu, Ikaho, Minakami (Gunma)

**Kusatsu Onsen:** No Shinkansen goes directly into Kusatsu — access is Limited Express from
Ueno to Naganohara-Kusatsuguchi Station, then a **bus transfer** into town, roughly **~3h
total** ([visit-gunma.jp](https://www.visit-gunma.jp/en/plan-your-trip/access-guide/how-to-get-to-kusatsu/)).
It IS accessible without a car, but the multi-leg transfer plus 3h+ each way is a full travel
day either direction, effectively forcing an overnight
([japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/kusatsu-onsen-day-trip-from-tokyo) explicitly
says "overnight recommended"). Kusatsu is one of Japan's most famous, highest-volume hot
spring towns.

**Ikaho Onsen:** Easier — Limited Express Ueno→Shibukawa then a short bus to Ikaho, **under
2.5h** ([visit-gunma.jp](https://www.visit-gunma.jp/en/plan-your-trip/access-guide/how-to-get-to-ikaho/)),
or Shinkansen Tokyo→Takasaki then bus. Fully car-free.

**Minakami:** Reachable via Shinkansen to Jomo-Kogen or local lines; a mix of onsen and (car-
optional) outdoor activities — but Minakami's biggest draws (canyoning, rafting, some gorge
walks) lean toward the "no serious hiking/adventure activity" line, so it's less aligned with
the couple's stated preferences unless they specifically want a gentler onsen-only visit there.

**Tattoo policy — important general finding:** A landmark Japan Tourism Agency survey found
**~56% of hotels/ryokan nationally still refuse tattooed guests outright**, ~31% allow tattoos,
and ~13% allow them only if covered with a sticker
([japanistry.com](https://www.japanistry.com/tattoo-policy-hot-springs/)). This is a national
baseline, not specific to Gunma — it reinforces that **private in-room or rentable private
baths are the reliable solution everywhere in this itinerary, not just "look for a
progressive facility."** Always book a room with its own bath or reserve a private rental
bath slot in advance; do not rely on walk-in access to communal baths anywhere, including
Kusatsu/Ikaho/Minakami.

**Verdict relative to Nikko exclusion:** Since Nikko itself is out of scope here, these three
Gunma towns are really "second-tier onsen day-trip-or-overnight" options competing with
Atami/Hakone. Given Kusatsu effectively requires an overnight and a long multi-leg transfer,
and Ikaho/Minakami are less famous/distinctive, **none of these beat Atami/Shuzenji or Hakone
for time-efficiency or onsen quality** — treat as backups only if the couple wants a
specifically Gunma/mountain-onsen flavor.

## 10. What's NEW in Tokyo since ~2019 (for a returning visitor)

The couple's last Tokyo visit predates most of these; all are genuinely new and don't require
leaving the city, so they can be woven into normal Tokyo days rather than costing "excursion"
time.

1. **Azabudai Hills** (Minato-ku) — opened Nov 2023, Mori Building's mixed-use complex
   (Japan's tallest building, Mori JP Tower), gardens, restaurants, and residence of teamLab
   Borderless. A genuinely new Tokyo skyline landmark.
2. **teamLab Borderless (Mori Building Digital Art Museum)** — reopened at Azabudai Hills
   **Feb 9, 2024** after its original Odaiba location closed; adult tickets ~**¥3,800–4,800**
   depending on date ([tokyoartbeat.com](https://www.tokyoartbeat.com/en/events/-/TeamLab-Borderless-Mori-Building-Digital-Art-Museum/747ED2E9/2024-02-09),
   [japaninsides.com](https://www.japaninsides.com/tokyos-beloved-teamlab-borderless-relocates-to-azabudai-hills-38602)).
   Different building and new installations from the pre-2019 Odaiba version.
3. **teamLab Planets** (Toyosu) — separate, larger-scale immersive/water-based installation,
   opened 2018 but has continued adding new works; pairs naturally with a Toyosu market visit.
4. **Toranomon Hills Station Tower** — completed 2023, part of the broader Toranomon-Azabudai
   redevelopment corridor; observation/dining floors, directly linked to a new subway station.
5. **Shibuya Sakura Stage** — opened 2023, part of the ongoing Shibuya station-area
   redevelopment (following Shibuya Scramble Square's 2019 opening, which the couple may also
   have missed depending on exact prior-trip timing).
6. **Shibuya Scramble Square / Shibuya Sky** — observation deck opened Nov 2019; likely still
   new to them if their prior trip predates late 2019.
7. **Harajuku redevelopment — Tokyu Plaza Harajuku "Harakado"** — opened 2024, replacing the
   older Tokyu Plaza building; fresh retail/dining draw right on Jingu-mae crossing.
8. **Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai** — opened **Feb 1, 2024**, an Edo-period-themed market complex of
   ~70 shops adjacent to Toyosu Fish Market, plus the **Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club**, a genuine
   **24-hour onsen and hotel facility** right in Tokyo
   ([gotokyo.org](https://www.gotokyo.org/en/spot/1858/index.html),
   [tokyoweekender.com](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/things-to-do-in-tokyo/toyosu-fish-market-tokyo-opens-onsen-complex/)).
   **Note for the tattoo constraint: this is a public bathing facility, not confirmed
   private-bath — verify Manyo Club's tattoo policy directly before planning a visit if onsen
   bathing (not just the market) is the goal.**
9. **Takanawa Gateway City** — new mixed-use district around JR's newest Yamanote-area
   station (Takanawa Gateway, opened 2020), continuing to develop through the mid-2020s.
10. **Yaesu / Tokyo Station redevelopment (Tokyo Torch, Tokiwabashi Tower)** — ongoing
    high-rise redevelopment directly beside Tokyo Station; new office/retail/observation space.
11. **Ghibli Museum remains in Mitaka** (pre-existing, not new) but worth flagging alongside
    the NEW Ghibli Park in Nagoya (see table above) for clarity that only the Mitaka museum is
    realistically Tokyo-day-trip-compatible.
12. **Sumida Hokusai Museum / other newer niche museums** — several smaller specialty museums
    have opened in Tokyo since 2019; worth a targeted look if the couple has a specific
    interest (art, design, etc.) closer to the trip.

## Ranking & Recommendation for the 11-night Tokyo Block

**Recommended top picks:**

1. **Hakone — 1 night.** Highest scenic/onsen payoff per unit of effort within pure train
   access. Use the 2-day Hakone Freepass, private-bath ryokan in Yumoto or Gora, spread the
   loop (Tozan railway, cablecar, ropeway, pirate ship, Open-Air Museum, and Owakudani if its
   live status is green) across the 2 days rather than rushing it in one. **Check Owakudani's
   live operational status a few days before travel — have a "skip it" fallback baked in.**

2. **Kamakura + Enoshima — 1 day trip.** Cheapest and easiest logistics on the whole list
   (<2h round trip transit), strong novelty (coastal tram, seaside shrine island, different
   texture from their Kyoto/Nara temple experience), no serious climbing beyond the manageable
   Enoshima slopes, and excellent food streets. This is close to a "free win" day.

3. **Atami or Shuzenji (Izu) — 1 night**, in preference to Naruko/Sendai. Same private-onsen
   payoff as a Tohoku trip but for a fraction of the travel time (under 1h vs. 2.5-3h each
   way), leaving far more net days in Tokyo. Recommend **Atami** specifically for simplicity
   (single Shinkansen leg, no further transfer) unless they want the extra half-step into Izu
   proper (Shuzenji town + Jogasaki coast walk) for more scenery.

**Optional 4th slot if they want more excursion variety:** Fuji Five Lakes / Kawaguchiko as a
day trip (skip the Chureito stairs if they don't feel like it, keep Oishi Park + the lake +
Momiji Corridor which is running during their exact dates), or a half-day Yokosuka+Yokohama
combined day using the same rail corridor.

**Suggested time budget out of 11 Tokyo nights:**
- Hakone: 1 night away (2 nights "lost" from pure Tokyo time only in the sense of being
  elsewhere, but it's a short hop back)
- Atami/Shuzenji: 1 night away
- Kamakura+Enoshima: 1 day trip (0 nights away, just a long day)
- Optional Fuji day trip or Yokosuka/Yokohama combined day: 1 more day trip (0 nights away)

That leaves **9 of 11 nights physically in Tokyo**, with 2 short, low-hassle overnights
(Hakone, Atami) that each cost under 1.5h of one-way travel — far more time-efficient than a
Naruko/Sendai trip would be, while still delivering genuine onsen-ryokan experiences distinct
from their first trip. Weave in the "new since 2019" Tokyo items (Azabudai Hills + teamLab,
Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai, Shibuya Sakura Stage) on ordinary Tokyo days rather than treating them
as separate excursions, since they cost no extra transit at all.

Naruko/Sendai and the Gunma onsen towns (Kusatsu/Ikaho/Minakami) are documented above as
solid backups if the couple specifically wants a "deeper into rural Japan" feel and is willing
to trade Tokyo time for it, but they don't outrank Hakone/Atami on a time-efficiency basis.
