## 1. RENTAL IS NOT AN OPTION — you buy

**Same-day walk-in Halloween costume rental for adults does not exist in Tokyo.** Verified across
English and Japanese sources. Tokyo's Halloween culture is purchase-based: adults buy cheap
costumes or improvise them.

What the "rental" market actually is, and why none of it works:

## 2. THE SHOPPING ROUTE

### Sizing is the real problem, and there's a clean answer

Japanese S/M will be picked over by afternoon. **Your advantage: LL/XL stock is far less
contested**, because Japanese customers overwhelmingly buy S/M.

Safest fits for European bodies:
- **Kigurumi onesies** — "free size" fits to ~180 cm, XL versions to 186 cm. Always deep stock
- **Inflatable costumes** — one-size by design (T-Rex, sumo). ¥3,000–5,000
- **Zentai / morphsuits** — stretch fabric, very forgiving
- **DIY** — black Uniqlo base plus Daiso accessories. Guaranteed fit

⚠️ **The "by noon" urgency is overstated.** Noon only matters for **specific licensed character costumes in Japanese S/M**, which won't fit you anyway. **Kigurumi, inflatables, morphsuits and generic costumes are stocked all day**, so **14:00–15:00 is fine**. Don't compress the afternoon for it.

⚠️ **Structured costumes will NOT fit EU L+ frames** — fitted jackets and dresses top out at JP L ≈ EU M. **Kigurumi is the safe bet.** Best insurance: **order a backup from Amazon Japan to the hotel before you fly** — 3XL is available from third-party sellers, 2-day delivery. Then Don Quijote becomes a fun browse rather than a single point of failure.

## 5. WARNINGS — read these

### Street drinking is banned where you'll be

| Ward | Scope | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Shinjuku** | Kabukicho 1-chome + Shinjuku 3-chome | **17:00 on 31 Oct → 05:00 on 1 Nov** ⚠️ *the 2024 ordinance text says 17:00. Event-specific, not year-round like Shibuya's — 2026 renewal not yet announced, re-check early Oct 2026* |
| **Shibuya** | All streets and parks near the station | 18:00–05:00, daily, year-round |
| **Minato (Roppongi)** | No ban | — |

No fines, but confiscation and verbal enforcement. **Drink inside venues.** Konbini in Shibuya and
Shinjuku may also be told to stop selling alcohol during restricted hours.

### Change at the hotel

There are **no temporary changing spaces** — the wards are discouraging costume gatherings, not
facilitating them. Station toilets are crowded and staff discourage lingering. A **manga cafe**
booth (¥500–1,000/hour) is the only decent backup, and they fill up on Halloween.

### Riding the train in costume

Not banned, but **strongly discouraged if gory.** Keio Railway formally asks passengers to refrain
from costumes that make others uneasy. Anime, pop culture and kigurumi are fine. Fake blood and
horror masks will draw stares and possibly staff intervention.

### ⚠️⚠️ TATTOOS — WARP may refuse you at the door

**WARP Shinjuku's official rules page states they may refuse entry to visible tattoos:**
「刺青・タトゥー（シールやペイントを含む）を露出されているお客様のご入場もお断りする場合がございます」
— *"Customers with exposed tattoos (including stickers and paint) may also be refused entry."*

This is the same constraint that has governed every onsen night on this trip, and nobody had
checked it against the club. On Halloween night staff are plausibly more lenient — everyone is in
costume — but **it is a stated policy, not a rumour, and it is not a risk worth arriving to test.**

**The clean answer is the costume you were already going to buy.** A **kigurumi is a full-body
onesie** — it covers everything, needs no concealer, and was already the recommendation for EU
sizing. One purchase solves sizing and the door policy together. If you'd rather not wear one,
long sleeves plus concealer is the fallback; a strapless or short-sleeved costume is not.

**If you are refused anyway:** Koenji's bars have no such policy and run late, and the manga-cafe
night pack in §4 covers the 00:30–05:00 rail gap. You are not stranded — you lose the club, not
the night.

### Safety — the two that matter

**Roppongi touts.** Still active and confirmed through 2025, concentrated on Gaien-Higashi Dori,
and **extra aggressive on Halloween.** The pattern is: lure into a bar → inflated bill of
¥30,000–100,000+ → intimidation. **Never follow a tout.** Say no and keep walking. Pick your
venues in advance. If you're trapped, insist on calling the police on **110**.

**Drink spiking.** Roppongi and Kabukicho are the documented hotspots — spiked drink,
unconsciousness, credit-card fraud. Never leave a drink unattended. If you feel suddenly and
extremely drunk after one or two drinks, leave immediately.

### Passport

**You are legally required to carry it at all times** — fine up to ¥100,000, and police ID checks
do happen on Halloween. Carry it in a pouch under the costume, and leave a photocopy at the hotel.
Do not put it in a club cloakroom.

### Smaller things

Coin lockers at Shibuya and Shinjuku will be **full** — try one station out (Yoyogi, Shin-Okubo).
**Ecbo Cloak** is app-based bag storage at shops, ¥400–700, reserve ahead. Ask before photographing
people: *shashin ii desu ka?* And Tokyo residential streets are library-quiet at 05:00 — keep it
down walking back.

**Emergency:** Police **110** · Ambulance/Fire **119** · JNTO tourist hotline (24/7 English)
**050-3816-2787** · koban police boxes are manned around the clock at major intersections.

**Kōenji (高円寺)** — 8 min JR from Shinjuku — has a real neighbourhood Halloween: costumed locals bar-hopping the shotengai and Enman-dōri, **no ordinance, no barriers, no cover, ¥500–800 beers**. ### Tokyo Disney Halloween — real, but it's an either/or

**Adults may wear full costumes only on designated days**, historically the last week of October including the 31st, and **Disney characters only** — no face-covering masks, no weapons over 50 cm. **¥10,900 pp = ¥21,800 for two**, on a day with **70,000–80,000 guests** and **120–180 min standby waits.**

The park *is* the attraction — you go to see thousands of people in costume, not to ride things. But it **closes at 21:00**, so you'd be home wrecked by 22:00. **It cannot coexist with the all-night plan.** Confirm costume-allowed dates at tokyodisneyresort.jp in September if you want it.

### Two expectations to drop

**There are no shrine or temple Halloween events.** Halloween isn't a Japanese religious or cultural observance — no temple does anything for it. And the **konbini/depachika seasonal goods** peak in the *second and third week* of October rather than on the 31st, so that's a whole-month pleasure, not a night-of one.

**The Kawasaki Halloween Parade — once Japan's biggest — is permanently cancelled, since 2021.**
