# Luggage Forwarding Strategy — North Line Leg (24 Oct–4 Nov 2026)

Planning only. No bookings made. Route: Sapporo → Hakodate (Yunokawa) → Hakodate Sta →
Sendai → Nikko town → Chuzenji (Hana-An) → Tokyo (11 nights).

---

## 1. How Takuhaibin works for foreign visitors

- **Where**: hotel front desk (ask for "Takkyubin" / "宅急便" or "宅配便") — no special
  procedure, no waybill preparation needed on your part; staff fill it in for you.
  [benrichards.co](https://benrichards.co/blog/ship-luggage-between-hotels-in-japan),
  [kuronekoyamato.co.jp](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/send/services/bothways/)
- **English support**: front desks in tourist hotels are used to this; airport counters
  (Narita/Haneda) are explicitly bilingual with English forms.
  [airtraveler.club](https://www.airtraveler.club/intel/japan-luggage-airport-hotel-13/)
  Smaller ryokan/onsen inns may need the destination hotel name/address written in
  Japanese — ask the sending hotel to help, or provide a printed Japanese address card
  for Hotel Hana-An in advance.
  [japantravel.com/JAL guide](https://ko.jal.japantravel.com/guide/takkyubin/31203)
- **Confirm the receiving hotel accepts early delivery** — always call/ask ahead,
  especially for the small Hana-An ryokan and the Nikko town hotel.
  [japantravel.com/JAL guide](https://ko.jal.japantravel.com/guide/takkyubin/31203)
- **Size/weight limits (Yamato TA-Q-BIN)**: max "Size 200" = sum of L+W+H ≤ 200cm,
  weight ≤ 30kg. Standard large 28"-30" checked suitcases are usually Size 160–180.
  [kuronekoyamato.co.jp rates](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/ytc/en/search/estimate/all_list.html)
- **Lead time, general rule**: next-day delivery for most Honshu city pairs; Hokkaido,
  Kyushu, Okinawa "usually take two days."
  [trulytokyo.com](https://trulytokyo.com/luggage-shipping-the-smart-way-to-travel-in-japan/)
  japan-guide.com is vaguer: "usually next day, except over very long distances, when
  it can take longer."
  [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2278.html)

### Lead times for THIS route's specific pairs
Yamato's public rate table is priced by region-pair and size, but does NOT publish an
official transit-time table by pair (only "next day standard, 2 days for
Hokkaido/Kyushu/Okinawa" as a general rule, confirmed above). Treat the below as
informed estimates layered on that rule — build in a 1-day safety buffer everywhere,
2 days for anything crossing the Tsugaru Strait (Hokkaido↔Honshu).

| Route | Same-island? | Expected transit | Buffer to plan for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sapporo → Hakodate (within Hokkaido) | Yes | 1 day | send 1 day ahead |
| Hakodate → Sendai (Hokkaido→Tohoku, crosses strait) | No | ~2 days | send 2 days ahead |
| Hakodate → Tokyo (Hokkaido→Kanto, crosses strait) | No | ~2 days | send 2 days ahead |
| Sendai → Nikko (Tohoku→Kanto, Honshu) | Yes | 1 day | send 1 day ahead |
| Sendai → Tokyo (Tohoku→Kanto, Honshu) | Yes | 1 day | send 1 day ahead |
| Nikko → Tokyo (Kanto→Kanto) | Yes | 1 day (often same/next day within Kanto) | send 1 day ahead |

The Hokkaido↔Honshu "crosses the strait" longer lead time is the one hard fact to
flag: Yamato's own rate structure treats Hokkaido as a distinct pricing zone from
every Honshu region (see table below), consistent with the extra ferry/rail-freight
leg. Build the Hakodate→Sendai and Hakodate→Tokyo sends with a full 2-day buffer, not
1 day, since a Halloween-morning delivery cannot afford to be late.

Per-suitcase, for 2 large suitcases (assume Size 160–180 bracket, which covers most

budget for the mobility gained.

drop-off, but hotel desk is far more convenient logistically for this itinerary — the
discount is not worth chasing.

## 3. Key recommendation — how to avoid dragging suitcases up Irohazaka

**Recommendation: forward both large suitcases from the Nikko town hotel directly to
the Tokyo hotel, and do Chuzenji (30 Oct) with overnight/day bags only.**

Why this is the right cut point, not Hakodate/Sendai→Tokyo directly:
- The binding constraint is the **Tobu bus up Irohazaka has genuinely limited luggage
  space** (see §6) — that is the one leg on this whole route where a large suitcase is
  a real physical problem, not just an inconvenience.
- Sending straight from Hakodate or Sendai all the way to Tokyo (skipping Nikko
  entirely) would mean carrying weeks' worth of clothes changes for Sendai→Nikko→
  Chuzenji with only a day bag, which is a bigger sacrifice than necessary — Nikko
  town itself is normal train + hotel, no luggage constraint.
- Cutting at Nikko keeps the couple's routine simple: pack a normal 1-night overnight
  bag for the Chuzenji night, take it up the bus, and the big suitcases are already
  waiting in Tokyo when they arrive the next afternoon (31 Oct).

**Exact days to send and lead time:**
- **Send from the Nikko town hotel on the morning of 30 Oct** (i.e., the day they
  move up to Chuzenji), addressed to the Tokyo hotel. Ask reception the evening of
  29 Oct or first thing 30 Oct before checkout — Yamato pickup/drop-off from a hotel
  is same-day, no advance reservation required.
- This is a Kanto→Kanto send (Nikko region → Tokyo/Kanto region), the fastest and
  most reliable lane on the whole route (next-day standard within Honshu, often even
  same-day within the Kanto/Tokyo delivery area).
  [trulytokyo.com](https://trulytokyo.com/luggage-shipping-the-smart-way-to-travel-in-japan/)
- Bags should be waiting at the Tokyo hotel by **31 Oct or, worst case, 1 Nov** —
  either way well before they need anything beyond overnight essentials, since they
  arrive at the Tokyo hotel around midday 31 Oct with only their day bags anyway
  after Chuzenji → Asakusa.
- **What they carry in the meantime (29–31 Oct)**: 1 small overnight/duffel bag per
  person from Nikko town → Chuzenji → Tokyo, containing a change of clothes,
  toiletries, onsen items, and Halloween-costume basics if compact enough (see the
  costume note below — full costume pieces from Spain may be worth keeping in the
  overnight bag specifically so they're on-hand exactly when reaching Tokyo, rather
  than waiting in a forwarded suitcase).

**Alternative/backup framing** — if the couple would rather not live out of a small bag
for 3 nights (Nikko town + Chuzenji + travel day), a second-best option is to forward
from **Sendai (28 Oct) straight to the Tokyo hotel**, and do Nikko town + Chuzenji with
day bags for all 3 remaining nights. This removes carry weight sooner but means living
out of overnight bags longer (3 nights instead of 1). Given Nikko town itself has no
transport constraint, the Nikko→Tokyo cut is the better balance of "minimize days
without full luggage" vs. "solve the actual Irohazaka bottleneck."

## 4. Does the same logic apply to the Yunokawa tram leg (26–27 Oct) and the 28 Oct Shinkansen day?

**Yunokawa tram (26–27 Oct): No forwarding needed.**
The Hakodate City Tram to Yunokawa Onsen is a normal city tram, not a mountain bus
with a luggage-space constraint — it's a ~30 min ride with normal aisle/floor space
for wheeled suitcases, same as any city tram/metro. There's no equivalent bottleneck
to Irohazaka here. The couple can simply wheel their suitcases on/off the tram from
Hakodate Station to the Yunokawa hotel and back to the hotel beside Hakodate Station
on 27 Oct. No takuhaibin needed for this hop.

**28 Oct Shinkansen day (Hakodate → Sendai): No forwarding strictly needed, but
consider it if the couple wants belt-and-braces convenience.**
Shinkansen has generous overhead/behind-seat luggage space and the reservation system
(§6) fully accommodates standard large suitcases with a free advance reservation. This
is not a bottleneck like Irohazaka. However, if the couple wants zero carrying at all
on this day, an optional additional forwarding leg is: **send from the Hakodate hotel
on 26 or 27 Oct directly to Sendai**, arriving the day before or the morning of the
28th, and travel Hakodate→Sendai with day bags only. This is a genuine option, not a
necessity — the Shinkansen leg is comfortable with full suitcases and reserved
oversized-baggage seating.

## 5. Coin lockers as an alternative

| Station | Large-locker availability (Oct) | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sapporo Station | Yes — small/medium/large tiers | ¥300–400 small, ¥400–500 medium, ¥600–700 large | [sapporo.travel](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/info/useful_information/how_to_use_coin-operated_lockers_in_japan/) |
| Hakodate (New Chitose Airport reference; Hakodate Station itself has fewer, smaller banks) | Limited — Hakodate is a smaller station, large lockers exist but in fewer numbers than Sapporo/Sendai; airport-side lockers ¥400–800 | ¥400–800 | [hokkaido-airports.com](https://www.hokkaido-airports.com/en/hakodate/service/baggage/locker/) |
| Sendai Station | Yes — major hub, ample large lockers | ~¥600–700 large (national norm) | [japan-experience.com](https://www.japan-experience.com/plan-your-trip/travel-by-train/japanese-stations/station-lockers-in-japan-a-comprehensive-guide-for-travelers) |
| Tobu Nikko Station | Yes — medium/large/extra-large tiers explicitly listed | ¥600 medium, ¥700 large, ¥900 extra-large | [nikkostation.com](https://www.nikkostation.com/lockers-and-baggage-storage-nikko-kinugawa-onsen-stations/) |
| Asakusa Station (Tobu) | Yes — small/medium/large | ¥300 small, ¥500 medium, ¥700 large | [asakusastation.com](https://www.asakusastation.com/lockers-baggage-storage-in-asakusa/) |

**Realistic fit for large (28"+) suitcases**: "Large" coin lockers nationwide are
typically sized around 86cm(H) × 34cm(W) × 65cm(D)
([navitime.com](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/guide/NTJhowto0076-en/)) —
this fits a 60L-ish bag but a genuinely large 30" hardside suitcase (often 45cm+ wide,
75cm+ tall) will NOT reliably fit even the "large" locker tier, especially at Hakodate
or Nikko where locker banks are smaller and inventory is limited. Coin lockers are a
good fit for day-trip use (Otaru day trip, Yunokawa day excursions) but are **not a
reliable substitute for takuhaibin when moving two genuinely large suitcases between
cities** — capacity risk is too high, particularly in October when leaf-viewing traffic
increases locker demand at Nikko and Sendai.
**Also note**: coin lockers only hold luggage 2–3 days max in practice before staff may
clear them; JAL ABC-style paid storage services allow weeks but aren't present at
every station on this route.
[jalabc.com](https://www.jalabc.com/en/hands-freetravel/baggage-storage.html)

**Verdict**: use coin lockers only for short same-day excursions (e.g., stashing a day
bag at Nikko Station while doing Toshogu on foot), not as the primary strategy for
inter-city luggage movement.

## 6. Bus/train luggage restrictions

**Shinkansen oversized-baggage rule — CONFIRMED it applies to the Hakodate→Sendai
Hayabusa leg.**
The rule (mandatory advance reservation, free, for bags with L+W+H between 160cm and
250cm; ¥1,000 fee if boarded without reservation) applies system-wide across
JR-operated Shinkansen lines, which includes the Hokkaido Shinkansen
(Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto) → Tohoku Shinkansen (→Sendai) Hayabusa service — this is one
continuous through-service on JR East/JR Hokkaido tracks, not a separate system exempt
from the rule.
[global.jr-central.co.jp](https://global.jr-central.co.jp/en/info/oversized-baggage/),
[jrpass.com](https://www.jrpass.com/blog/shinkansen-luggage-rules-size-limits-oversized-bags-and-reservations-explained)
Most large 28"-30" hardside suitcases (typically ~160cm combined dimensions) sit right
at or above this threshold — **reserve the oversized-baggage seats for the Hayabusa
Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai leg** when booking, for both suitcases. It's free but must
be requested at time of ticket purchase (JR ticket machine, counter, or online
reservation system).
[japan-experience.com](https://www.japan-experience.com/plan-your-trip/to-know/before-you-travel/takkyubin-ship-your-luggage-between-destinations-in-japan)

**Tobu limited express (Nikko↔Asakusa, and the Spacia/Spacia X/Revaty services):**
Tobu's own English luggage-specific policy page does not publish an oversized-baggage
reservation system analogous to JR Shinkansen's — Tobu limited express trains have
standard overhead racks and end-of-car luggage space but no seat-linked size
reservation requirement was found in Tobu's official English pages.
[tobu.co.jp Spacia info](https://www.tobu.co.jp/en/express_info/express/spacia.html)
In practice this means: no special reservation needed for 2 large suitcases on the
Tobu limited express itself, but rack/luggage-area space is first-come, and Spacia X
in particular is popular and can sell out (per prior trip research — book the 30 Sept
09:00 JST window). If the timing works out that the Nikko→Tokyo forwarding leg (§3)
removes the suitcases before this ride is needed, it becomes moot for the return leg;
if not forwarding, board early to secure luggage space.

**Tobu bus up Irohazaka to Chuzenji — genuinely constrained, confirmed.**
Tobu buses on this route are standard-size sightseeter/local buses, not
airport-limousine coaches with a dedicated cargo hold. Luggage space is limited to
what fits in the aisle/rack area or a small under-seat hold — nothing like the ~1m³
underfloor cargo bays that intercity coaches (e.g. Limousine Bus, which explicitly
limits items to 50×60×120cm max
[limousinebus.co.jp](https://www.limousinebus.co.jp/uploads/info/2025/10/eb160de1de89d9058fcb0b968dbbbd68.pdf))
have. Two large 28-30" suitcases plus two people's day bags on a switchback mountain
road bus, likely standing-room-busy in peak autumn-color season, is the scenario to
avoid — this confirms the user's instinct and is the central reason for the
recommendation in §3.
[tobu.co.jp bus info](https://www.tobu.co.jp/en/access/nikko/bus_nikko.html)
