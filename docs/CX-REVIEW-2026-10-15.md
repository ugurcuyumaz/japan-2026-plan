# Japan planner CX review — 15 October 2026

## What the planner needs to do

Help two travelers prepare for the booked Japan trip, quickly find the right day while traveling, understand what is fixed versus optional, and move from a place to directions or a reservation without losing context. All hotels and flights are confirmed; other arrangements remain open. The trip facts and researched alternatives are retained.

## Map behavior

Selecting a base reveals its dates, booked hotels and itinerary days. Selecting a place reveals its research and actions. Selecting a group reveals all of its stops, including exact coordinate duplicates. The overview initially fits the whole route; day maps fit the local visit while retaining long-distance endpoints in the place list. A dashed line means stop order, not a verified walking, driving or rail path. Google Maps directions remain the navigation action.

The vector renderer is loaded separately from the main planner. Tile and style availability depends on the external map service; itinerary content and place links remain usable without it.

## Second pass: break the experience down by traveler intent

The next review looked at the complete sequence: prepare at home → choose a date → make a Tokyo choice → get to a stop → return to the correct hotel → share the plan with the other traveler. The largest opportunities were between sections, rather than another change of colors.

| Traveler intent | Keep | Friction found | Improvement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Understand the trip | Compact opening, confirmed hotels/flights, route and next actions | More decoration would compete with useful controls | Keep the visual direction; add practical clarity inside the tools |
| Open an arrival day | Six route visits in order | The return-Tokyo button searched an exact base name and could open Kamakura on 5 Nov | Derive each arrival from the booked hotel dates, combining contiguous same-city stays; Tokyo return now opens 3 Nov |
| Use a day on the move | One chronological plan, alternatives and contingencies | A large day map and place list pushed the actual itinerary far down | Collapse and mount the day map on demand; selecting an itinerary stop opens the map automatically |
| Find tonight’s hotel | Confirmed hotel research and map links | Hotel context was mixed into all the stops, including both hotels on a move day | Add an at-a-glance card distinguishing tonight, check-in and checkout, with hotel directions and details |
| Prepare for a particular date | Official booking board and honest statuses | A traveler had to cross-reference the date with a separate board | Add that day’s open advance actions and an on-site purchase count; action buttons open and focus the matching booking task |
| Choose an idea for an open day | One main outing plus an optional evening | The day-to-pool action lost its date; each activity demanded another date selection | Carry the chosen day into the idea browser, open it and preselect the date; keep a global planning-date control |
| Share choices across two travelers/devices | Browser persistence without an account | A day URL did not transfer the personal Tokyo selections | Add snapshot links with a full calendar preview, explicit replacement, undo and clear explanation that later changes do not sync |
| Trust imported choices | Known itinerary and researched activities | Browser data was accepted without validating dates, activity kinds or duplicate choices | Validate saved/shared data; invalid links leave current choices unchanged |
| Avoid an impossible evening | Flexible choices remain optional | Long outings could run into the izakaya evening when merged into one timeline | Warn before assignment and in the calendar/day view when the outing leaves under 30 minutes or overlaps the evening; the warning is based on planning blocks, not live transport |
| Start from the right Tokyo hotel | Existing researched routes | All pool routes used Comfort Hotel, including 1 Nov during the Ikebukuro stay | Rebase hotel endpoints, coordinates and Google Maps origins/destinations to the booked hotel; remove invalid transfer durations and prominently request a live timing check |
| Find food or a sight on the main map | Informative pins, details panel and external directions | Whole-trip layers could contain many far-apart places | Add place search, itinerary-base filtering, result counts and a useful empty state |
| Navigate on a phone | Focus trap and previous/next controls | Closing a long day required returning to the top; menu labels came from internal anchor names | Keep the close button reachable while scrolling and use human-readable navigation labels |

### Deliberate limits

Snapshot sharing is not live collaboration. Map lines are not turn-by-turn navigation. Timing warnings use the researched planning blocks; they do not confirm opening dates, transport connections or reservations. The earlier Tokyo hotel requires a fresh route-time check. Confirmed booking status remains based on the travelers’ actual confirmations. No budget ceiling is invented.

The focused automated suite now includes hotel-change/checkout boundaries, both Tokyo arrival dates, snapshot round-trips and rejection of invalid data, late-evening conflicts, and correct hotel endpoints without mutating the researched activity templates. Live desktop and mobile checks verify the new interactions after deployment.
