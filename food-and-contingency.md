Planning only. No reservations made. Covers: Sapporo (24–25 Oct), Hakodate (26–27 Oct),
Sendai (28 Oct, Wed), Nikko (29 Oct), Lake Chuzenji (30 Oct).

### SAPPORO

Small-counter high-end sushi in Japan generally books ahead — for a splurge dinner, book 2–3 days out via Tabelog's English reservation button where available, or via hotel concierge. Kaisendon (rice-bowl) versions at market stalls are walk-in. |
| Uni and scallops | **No reservation — plan by season/location, see Part B** | Best obtained via market kaisendon stalls (Nijo/Curb) or an izakaya/sushi-ya specializing in Hokkaido seafood — not a bookable "experience" as such. |

**Sapporo bottom line:** almost everything is walk-in-and-queue culture. The **only** things worth actively trying to book ahead are (a) a splurge sushi omakase counter, and (b) Daruma jingisukan if going on a weekend evening.

### LAKE CHUZENJI (30 Oct 2026)

Food options at the lake are genuinely limited outside ryokan half-board, and several sources flag that dining infrastructure is seasonal/soba-and-yuba-snack-tier rather than destination dining:

- **Oyasumi Dokoro Kiyo-chan** — a casual spot near the lake known specifically for yuba soba with tableside fish-grilling as a bit of visual theatre ([japantravel.com](https://en.japantravel.com/tochigi/yuba-soba-at-lake-chuzenji/59420)). Walk-in, casual, good for a quick lakeside lunch.
- **Teuchi Soba Katsura** — a handmade-soba restaurant directly on the Chuzenji lakeshore ([gltjp.com](https://cdn.gltjp.com/en/directory/item/15039/)). Walk-in; a legitimate sit-down lunch option.
- **Cafes/food stalls near the Kegon Falls elevator office** — burgers, ice cream, yuba snacks; casual, walk-in, more a snack stop than a meal ([nikkostation.com](https://www.nikkostation.com/kegon-falls/)).
- The Ritz-Carlton Nikko's Japanese restaurant (not lakeside, but in the wider Okunikko/Chuzenji area) takes lunch 12:00–15:00 — an option if you want a higher-end sit-down meal and don't mind the price tier and being slightly off the lake itself ([nikkostation.com](https://www.nikkostation.com/the-ritz-carlton-nikko/)).

**Verdict: yes, there is a real lunch option on 30 Oct** — Teuchi Soba Katsura or a yuba-soba stall near the lake pier/Kegon Falls area covers it, all walk-in. Don't expect variety or a food "experience" comparable to the other bases; treat lunch at Chuzenji as simple and functional, and let dinner (ryokan half-board at Hotel Hana-An) carry the culinary weight of the day.

## PART B — Seasonal food, late October

Confirmed via multiple independent sources:

| Item | In season late Oct? | Source-confirmed timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Salmon (aki-zake / autumn salmon) and ikura (salmon roe)** | **Yes — squarely in season** | Multiple sources converge on **September–November** as the Hokkaido autumn-salmon/ikura window ([myeasternhokkaido.com](http://www.myeasternhokkaido.com/blog/hokkaido-seafood), [sushiliv.com](https://sushiliv.com/types/ikura/), [matcha-jp.com](https://matcha-jp.com/en/12918)); one source even narrows peak salmon-fishing/ikura-don season to **Sept–Oct specifically** ([foodinjapan.org](https://foodinjapan.org/hokkaido/ikura-don/)). Late Oct is prime time — this is the single best seasonal ask of the trip. |
| **Hanasaki crab** | **Yes, but right at the tail end** | Official japan.travel source states Hanasaki crab is in season **August–October**, unlike the other three Hokkaido crabs which peak in winter ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/guide/cheap-and-delicious-seafood-hokkaido/)). One French-language source instead gives **March–September** for Hanasaki ([japan-experience.com](https://www.japan-experience.com/plan-your-trip/to-know/japanese-food/winter-culinary-specialties-made-with-zuwai-crab)) — **sources disagree on the exact end date**; the safest read is "available but possibly past its best by 24 Oct–1 Nov, ask specifically for it and expect it may already be off some menus." Don't build the trip's crab expectations around Hanasaki alone. |
| **King crab (tarabagani) and snow crab (zuwaigani)** | **No — winter peak, confirmed NOT in season** | Consistent across sources: king crab peaks **Sept/Nov–Jan/Feb** ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/my/delicious-japan/hokkaido/), [savorjapan.com](https://savorjapan.com/contents/discover-oishii-japan/indulge-in-crab-japan-s-ultimate-winter-luxury-food/)); snow crab peaks **Nov–March** ([omakaseje.com](https://omakaseje.com/articles/cz183917)). Late Oct is the shoulder before the real season starts — it will be available (frozen/farmed supply chains make crab available year-round in restaurants) but **not at its best, and pricier for the quality**. Don't prioritize king/snow crab this trip. |
| **Scallops (hotate)** | **Weak season, not the best window** | Multiple sources put scallop peak at **December–February** ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/guide/cheap-and-delicious-seafood-hokkaido/)); a kaisendon-focused source instead lists scallops as a **spring** ingredient ([jeepe.jp](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/hokkaido-kaisendon-guide-1338)). Either way, **late October is not scallop's peak** — still perfectly good and available, just not the seasonal highlight. |
| **Uni (sea urchin)** | **Off-peak — sources disagree but converge on summer, not autumn** | Official japan.travel page states uni season is **June–August** ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/sg/guide/cheap-and-delicious-seafood-hokkaido/)); a kaisendon-specific source agrees, putting uni in the **summer** category alongside salmon and squid ([jeepe.jp](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/hokkaido-kaisendon-guide-1338)). **Verdict: uni is not a late-October highlight** — it will be on menus (often stored/frozen or sourced from elsewhere in Japan) but this is not the season to prioritize it. |
| **Oysters** | **Not specifically confirmed for Hokkaido in this pass** | No source in this research batch gave an explicit Hokkaido oyster season window. General Japan oyster knowledge (outside this research) skews winter (Nov–Feb) for most regions — treat as **likely off-peak or just starting** in late Oct; verify locally if this matters to you, don't build plans around it. |

### What to specifically order this week (24 Oct–1 Nov)

- **Hokkaido (Sapporo/Otaru/Hakodate): ikura and aki-zake (autumn salmon)** — ask for it by name (イクラ / 秋鮭) at any kaisendon counter or market stall; this is the one genuinely "perfect timing" seasonal ask of the whole trip.
- **Hanasaki crab** if you see it on a menu — ask, don't assume it's automatically offered; it may be tailing off.
- Don't specifically chase king crab, snow crab, scallops, or uni this week — order them if you want them (they're still good, year-round supply chains exist), but don't treat them as "must eat now because it's the season," because it isn't.
- **Tohoku (Sendai): sasa-kamaboko** is not seasonal — a year-round craft item, good any time you visit.

## PART C — Bad-weather and contingency plan

### SAPPORO (24–25 Oct)

Sapporo has one of the most extensive indoor pedestrian networks in Japan — a genuinely strong rainy-day city.

1. **Chi-Ka-Ho underground walkway + Aurora Town/Pole Town underground malls** — connects Sapporo Station to Susukino (a ~15-min walk) entirely undercover, lined with shops, event spaces, and a small aviary feature (shell parakeets) at Aurora Town's "Otori Plaza" ([sapporo.travel](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/spot/facility/sapporo_underground_shopping_malls/), [tourismattractions.net](https://tourismattractions.net/japan/best-things-to-do-in-sapporo-in-winter)). Free to walk, always open during shopping-center hours.
2. **Shiroi Koibito Park** — chocolate-factory theme park with a projection-mapping show and hands-on cookie/chocolate workshops. Hours **10:00–17:30 (last admission 16:30)**; general admission **¥800 adults / ¥400 children** (some sources cite a broader ¥2,000 course option) ([shiroikoibitopark.jp](https://www.shiroikoibitopark.jp/en/about.html), [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e5307.html)). Fully indoor, good for 1–1.5 hours.
3. **Sapporo Beer Museum** — free museum on brewing history, attached restaurant hours **11:00–18:30 (LO 18:00)** ([sapporobeer.jp](https://www.sapporobeer.jp/english/brewery/s_museum/)). Combine with jingisukan lunch/dinner there (see Part A) as an indoor two-for-one.
4. **JR Tower Observatory T38** — 160m-high indoor observation deck directly attached to Sapporo Station, a good indoor option specifically when it's foggy/rainy but you still want a city view ([sapporo.travel](https://www.sapporo.travel/en/spot/facility/chikaho/)).
5. **Department store food halls / Odori Bisse** — accessible via the underground network, good for an indoor browse-and-snack hour.
6. Maruyama Zoo is only a partial fallback — flagging that **much of it is outdoors**, so it's a weak choice specifically for a rain/wind day; skip it as a bad-weather pick.

### OTARU (day trip from Sapporo)

1. **Otaru Canal + Sakaimachi Street glassworks and music box halls** — the glass and music-box shops themselves are indoor and browsable even if the canal walk is rained out.
2. **Nikka Yoichi Distillery** — reachable via JR Hakodate Line from Sapporo/Otaru to Yoichi Station (~40 min–1h15 depending on source, [nikka.com](https://nikka.com/en/distilleries/yoichi/access/), [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1928/)); **trains on this line run infrequently** — Nikka's own FAQ explicitly warns "there are few trains operating between Otaru Station and Yoichi Station" ([nikka.com](https://nikka.com/en/distilleries/yoichi/access/)), so this requires checking the timetable in advance and building slack into the day, not a spontaneous add-on. Guided tours accept reservations for groups of 1–9 and include a free tasting (English/Chinese/Japanese guiding available via some tour operators, [nikka.com FAQ](http://nikka.com/en/distilleries/yoichi/faq/), [kkday.com](https://www.kkday.com/en-id/product/165321)). **Given the train-frequency problem, a half/full-day guided bus tour from Sapporo (several operators found, e.g. kkday) may be more reliable than DIY train for a single-day visit.** Fully indoor once there — good rain fallback.
3. Otaru Sushi Street is walk-in-friendly (one prominent shop, Masazushi, explicitly allows walk-in or same-day booking despite recommending reservations, [lastminutejapan.com](https://lastminutejapan.com/sushi_otaru_masazushi)) — a solid indoor lunch regardless of weather.

### HAKODATE (26–27 Oct)

**Mt. Hakodate ropeway wind/fog closure — the real numbers:**
- The ropeway suspends service when wind speed exceeds a threshold or in generally bad weather; multiple sources confirm this happens periodically but none gave hard statistics on how many days per year/month it's actually closed ([hakodatestation.com](https://www.hakodatestation.com/hakodate-station-to-mt-hakodate/) — "sometimes suspended due to high winds"). **No precise frequency figure was found for Mt. Hakodate specifically in this research pass** — treat "sometimes" as the honest answer rather than inventing a percentage.
- **When the ropeway is closed, there is no reliable alternative to the summit in this season.** Private vehicles/taxis are barred from the summit road in winter (road closed to motor vehicles Nov–Apr per one source, [ana.co.jp](https://www.ana.co.jp/en/jp/japan-travel-planner/hokkaido/0000006.html)); the shuttle bus to the ropeway station only runs **April 1–October 31** ([uu-hokkaido.jp](http://map.uu-hokkaido.jp/e/hakodate_ropeway/)) — so **by very late Oct you're on the edge of the shuttle-bus season window too**. Walking up is technically possible for the fit but not realistic as a real "backup plan" for most travelers.
- **Practical fallback if the ropeway is closed: Goryokaku Tower** — an indoor observation tower over the star-fort park, a genuinely good substitute view experience and fully weatherproof.
- Other indoor options: Hakodate Museum of Northern Peoples, the Old Public Hall of Hakodate Ward, and Hakodate History Plaza — all indoor, all viable on a wet day. (Named from general Hakodate tourism knowledge; specific hours/prices were not independently re-verified in this pass — check current hours before relying on any single one.)
- **The Morning Market itself is a good rain fallback** — it's a covered market building, so a rainy morning doesn't really disrupt the squid-fishing/donburi plan at all.

### SENDAI (28 Oct)

- **Sendai Umino-Mori Aquarium** — a large, mostly-indoor aquarium with dolphin/sea-lion shows, a food court, and gift shops; explicitly recommended as a rainy-day pick ([jeepe.jp](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/sendai-uminomori-aquarium-1969), [wenthetravelbegins.com](https://wenthetravelbegins.com/en/sendai-aquarium-en/)). Note: it's not right at the station — a free shuttle exists from Nakanosakae Station, though exact schedule wasn't confirmed in this pass ([tripadvisor.com](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attractions-g298249-Activities-zft11295-Sendai_Miyagi_Prefecture_Tohoku.html)).
- **Underground/covered shopping arcades near Sendai Station** — Hapina Nakakecho, Clis Road, and Sunmall Ichibancho shopping streets connect together, letting you browse and eat undercover without ever stepping into rain ([gltjp.com](https://cdn.gltjp.com/en/article/item/21480/)). Given you're only there one evening (28 Oct, en route Hakodate→Nikko), this covered-arcade network is actually the most *practically relevant* fallback — it's exactly where the gyutan restaurants are anyway.
- Given the short one-evening stay in Sendai, a full museum visit is likely unnecessary — the covered arcades + station-adjacent gyutan dinner already form a self-contained indoor plan regardless of weather.

### NIKKO and LAKE CHUZENJI (29–30 Oct)

**Lake Chuzenji sightseeing boat cruise in bad weather:**
- No source in this research pass gave an explicit "the boat cancels in rain X% of the time" statistic for Lake Chuzenji specifically. What was found is more concerning than a simple rain risk: **recent reporting shows Lake Chuzenji has experienced drought-driven low water levels severe enough that of the four boat piers, only one remains operational** ([gettyimages.com — stock footage caption](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/video/pier-stands-at-lake-chuzenji-where-water-levels-have-news-footage/2278228288)). This is a **structural availability risk independent of weather on the day** — **verify closer to the date whether the cruise is running at all**, not just whether it's raining. Treat the boat cruise as a "nice to have if operating" rather than a locked-in plan.
- **If the cruise isn't running or weather is bad:** Kegon Falls has elevator access (low-effort, confirmed accessible regardless of most weather), and a lakeside walk near the Chuzenji Onsen base area is doable in light rain with an umbrella. Route recommendation from research: treat the eastern lake shore (bus stops, restaurants, falls access, piers) as your base and do 2–3 add-ons rather than trying to circle the whole lake ([japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/nikko/lake-chuzenji)).

**Irohazaka road (Nikko ↔ Chuzenji) in heavy rain or early snow:**
- Late October is actually the **worst-traffic** window for a different reason than weather — it's peak autumn-foliage season, and multiple sources warn of heavy congestion on weekends/holidays specifically in **mid-to-late October through early November** ([japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e3816.html), [visitnikko.jp](https://www.visitnikko.jp/en/spots/irohazaka-slope/)). **30 Oct 2026 is a Friday** — check this against the standard advice to "avoid weekends and holidays," since a Friday sits right at the edge of weekend traffic building up.
- On actual weather closures: winter closures and chain requirements are well documented ([tripbase.com](https://www.tripbase.com/destinations/nikko-japan/when-to-visit/), [jeepe.jp](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/irohazaka-nikko-guide-1674)) but these references are to **winter** (Dec–Mar) conditions, not late October. **No source confirmed an actual history of Irohazaka closures specifically in late October** — early snow at this elevation (Chuzenji sits at 1,269m) is not impossible this early, but it would be an anomaly, not the norm, for this exact week. You are almost certainly dealing with rain/fog risk, not a snow-closure risk, on 29–30 Oct.
- **You will be on Tobu buses, not driving yourself** — no chains/self-driving risk applies to you directly; the practical risk is delay from congestion or, in the event of a genuine early-season snow event, a bus schedule disruption. Build slack into the 29→30 Oct transition day rather than a tight connection.

**Nikko/Chuzenji indoor fallback for a rainy day:**
- Toshogu itself has substantial covered/roofed structures (worth noting you won't be fully exposed even touring the shrine in light rain), plus an on-site museum.
- Yubatei Masudaya or another sit-down yuba-kaiseki lunch (see Part A) doubles as an indoor weather refuge.
- Ritz-Carlton Nikko's restaurant (12:00–15:00 lunch) is a viable indoor sit-down option in the wider Okunikko area if you want to wait out heavier rain over a longer lunch.
