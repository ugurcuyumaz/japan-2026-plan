| ~15:50 | **MT HAKODATE SUMMIT BUS** ⭐ — **not the ropeway, which is almost certainly closed.** Every 10 min from 15:50, **¥500 one way**, returns until 19:40. **Runs rain or shine** |
| ~16:20 | On the summit for **sunset (~16:40–16:50)** and the blue hour after it |
| Evening | **Shio ramen at Ajisai (味彩) in the station building** — founded 1930, on Tabelog's Hokkaido Ramen 100 for 2025, and **thirty seconds from your bed**. Salt, not the miso you had in Sapporo |

⚠️ **THE ROPEWAY IS ALMOST CERTAINLY CLOSED, AND THE REPLACEMENT IS BETTER.** Mt Hakodate ropeway shuts for **three to four weeks every autumn** for its statutory inspection (法定整備点検) — **2024: 18 Oct – 10 Nov. 2025: 9 Oct – 9 Nov.** Your dates sit squarely inside that window in both years.

**The summit bus removes the single point of failure this day used to carry.** The ropeway is wind-dependent and closes without notice; the bus **runs rain or shine**. So the 28th no longer needs to hold a backup evening, and you no longer need a Goryokaku Tower consolation plan. It also flips a money decision: on the bus, the **¥1,400 bus-and-tram day pass pays for itself on day one** — two summit-bus legs plus two tram rides is ¥1,520 bought separately.

**Confirm the exact 2026 dates on 334.co.jp in September** — they're announced two to four weeks ahead. If by some chance the ropeway *is* running, take it; the bus is the plan either way.

⚠️ **Two of the shio-ramen shortlist are shut on your days.** **Jiyoken (自由軒) is closed Tuesday, Wednesday AND Thursday** — all three of your Hakodate days. **Ho Ran (鳳蘭) is closed Tuesday**, your arrival day. Ajisai is open and is the right arrival-night answer anyway.

⚠️ **THE 11:00 HOKUTO 8 IS THE DAY'S REAL DEADLINE.** Noboribetsu has exactly **two** southbound Hokuto services in your window: **Hokuto 8 at 11:00 → Hakodate 13:35**, and **Hokuto 10 at 12:09 → 14:40.** There is nothing earlier. Take the 11:00 and you have **two comfortable hours** for Motomachi before the 15:50 summit bus; miss it and Motomachi becomes a **60-minute dash.** **Leave the onsen by 10:15** — shuttle if the timing works, ¥1,000 taxi if it doesn't. Do not treat this as flexible.

*Sunset is given as a range because sources differ and neither figure comes from a direct tabular lookup. Two independent interpolations both land at **~16:42**, so the 16:40–16:50 range is sound — but being on the summit by 16:20 covers either.*

**Not in season:** **seri-nabe (セリ鍋)**, the Sendai winter dish everyone recommends, doesn't start until November–December. Don't go looking for it.

| Evening | **Second Kokubuncho evening** — **Iroha Yokocho** or a local izakaya. Sanma, sashimi, a Miyagi sake flight: **Urakasumi (浦霞)**, **Hidakami (日高見)**, **Ichinokura (一ノ蔵)**. ¥5,000–7,000 for two |

✅ **Built on the 12:00 cruise deliberately, not the 11:00.** The 11:00 sailing was feasible on paper but it capped **Zuiganji at 45 minutes** — and Zuiganji is the largest Zen temple in northern Japan, where the cave corridors alone want 10–15 minutes and unhurried viewing wants 50–70. **The 12:00 buys Zuiganji 75 minutes and removes the only cascading link in the day.** You get back to Sendai at ~16:10 instead of 15:30, which costs nothing — the evening is a Kokubuncho izakaya night either way.

The 08:15 departure genuinely arrives by **~08:55** ✅, and the **12:00 sailing runs even on the most restrictive winter timetable** (9:00–15:00), so it's safe regardless of when the operator starts calling late October "winter." **The 11:00 remains available** if you happen to move fast through Zuiganji — you'd just gain an hour at the end.

⚠️ **THE OYSTER HUTS WILL BE SHUT.** かき小屋 season opens **late November to early December** — Matsushima Bay magaki are harvested from November onward. The signature charcoal all-you-can-eat huts will not be operating on 30 October. Individual restaurants serve oysters à la carte year-round, but that isn't the experience. **Anago is the equally-regional replacement.**

⚠️ **THE ENTSUIN ILLUMINATION IS CUT.** 2026 dates won't be published until September or October; historical starts are **27 Oct (2023)** and **28 Oct (2022)**, so the 30th probably falls inside the window — but three things argue against building the evening on it. It is **an autumn-colour light-up**, which you've said not to weight. The mechanics are poor: you'd be back in Sendai at 15:30 and would have to return to Matsushima, **80 minutes of round-trip train for a 45-minute experience** on a day you've already spent six hours there. And it's a **separate ¥500 pp evening admission** on top of the daytime ticket you've already used.

**You see Entsuin's gardens in daylight either way.** Without the return trip the day is well-paced rather than over-packed, and the evening becomes a proper Kokubuncho night. If you'd rather keep the option, check **matsushima-kanko.com** in early October and treat it as a bonus.

**On the combination ticket:** Zuiganji ¥700 + Entsuin ¥500 = ¥1,200, and the advertised combo is *also* ¥1,200 — so by those figures it **saves nothing**. Ask at the first window; don't assume a discount.

**Do not plan a one-way Shiogama → Matsushima cruise** — that route has been discontinued or sporadic since COVID. Round trip from Matsushima pier only.

**Nikko:** Lake Chuzenji, Kegon Falls, the Embassy Villas, Irohazaka. Two nights with a midday Monday arrival leave one full day and the shrine complex takes it. The lake cruise is probably not running (drought left one of four piers operational), Irohazaka jams reach 2–3 hours on holidays and buses aren't exempt, and **Akechidaira Ropeway is closed Jan 2026 – Sep 2027**.
