# Transport Costs & Rail Pass Feasibility — Sapporo→Hakodate→Sendai→Nikko leg (24-30 Oct 2026)

Fixed itinerary, 2 adults, Madrid-based, no rental car. All fares are per-person, one-way, in JPY unless noted. Adult standard/ordinary-car fares; child/Green Car omitted unless relevant.

**Bottom line up front:** buy point-to-point tickets for this leg. No single pass — and no *combination* of two passes — beats cash fares for this specific 7-night, one-directional itinerary, because it crosses the JR Hokkaido / JR East company boundary exactly once (at Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto) and never doubles back over any single stretch of track. Passes earn their price by being used for **round trips or many highway-scale one-way hops within one company's territory**; this itinerary is a single ~1,400 km one-way transect through 2-3 pass territories, ridden once each way. See §2 for the arithmetic.

### 2.2 The break-even math

**Point-to-point cash total for the JR-network portion of this itinerary (legs 4-8, the segments any of the wide-area passes could possibly cover):**

- Sapporo→Hakodate (Hokuto): ¥9,440–9,770
- Hakodate→Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto (local): ~¥400 (estimate, unverified exact yen)
- Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai (Hayabusa): ¥18,300
- Sendai→Utsunomiya (Yamabiko): ¥8,150
- Utsunomiya→Nikko (local): ~¥800

**Cash total per person ≈ ¥37,090–37,420** for exactly those 5 rail legs, ridden once each, no round trips, no side trips squeezed in.

Compare against the passes that *could* cover the whole span:

- **JR Tohoku-South Hokkaido Pass — ¥32,000.** Covers legs 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 in full (all are JR / JR-Hokkaido lines within its area) but **not** leg 9-11 (Tobu is non-JR and outside this pass's non-JR exception list). **¥32,000 pass vs ¥37,090-37,420 cash for the same 5 legs → saves ~¥5,000-5,400 per person**, i.e. **this is the one pass that clears break-even on its own network**, and it does so without needing the Otaru round trip or CTS-Sapporo leg thrown in (those are Hokkaido-only and this pass covers Hokkaido too, as a bonus — see below).
- **JR East-South Hokkaido Pass — ¥40,000.** Covers everything the Tohoku-South Hokkaido pass covers, PLUS the Tobu Nikko↔Asakusa limited express (both base fare and reserved seat, per the explicit non-JR exception clause) PLUS the CTS-Sapporo Rapid Airport train and the Sapporo-Otaru round trip (all JR East/JR Hokkaido lines within its area). Total point-to-point equivalent for what it covers: ¥37,090-37,420 (rail legs above) + ¥1,150-1,570 (CTS-Sapporo) + ¥3,600 (Otaru round trip) + ~¥3,050-3,940 (Tobu Nikko-Asakusa) ≈ **¥44,890-46,530 cash vs ¥40,000 pass → saves ~¥4,900-6,500 per person**, and it is the *only single pass that reaches every rail/Tobu leg in this itinerary end-to-end*.
- **JR Hokkaido Rail Pass (5 or 7-day) — ¥22,000/¥28,000.** Explicitly excludes the Hokkaido Shinkansen, so it cannot touch your single most expensive leg (Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai, ¥18,300). It only ever covers Sapporo→Hakodate (¥9,440-9,770), Hakodate→Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto (~¥400), CTS→Sapporo (¥1,150-1,570) and the Otaru round trip (¥3,600) — **¥14,590-15,340 of coverage for a ¥22,000 pass. Loses money by ¥6,600-7,400 per person.** Not viable for this itinerary shape (it's built for people doing loops around Hokkaido, not a one-way transect out of it).
- **New JR East Pass (¥35,000 / 5-day)** — Hokkaido is entirely outside its territory, so it cannot touch legs 4-6 at all (your single biggest chunk of spend, ¥27,740-28,070 combined). It could only ever cover Sendai→Utsunomiya→Nikko (¥8,950 combined) for ¥35,000. **Catastrophic mismatch — loses ~¥26,000 per person.** Do not buy this for this itinerary; it would only make sense on a trip that stays entirely within Tohoku/Kanto/Nagano-Niigata.
- **Nationwide 7-day JR Pass (¥53,000 post-Oct-2026, or up to ~¥56,600 if bought via an overseas resale agent under the new dual-pricing rule)** — covers every JR leg (4-8) AND the Tobu Nikko-Asakusa exception, matching the JR East-South Hokkaido pass's reach but costing ¥13,000-16,600 *more* for identical coverage on this itinerary, because you have no other long-haul JR legs elsewhere in Japan to soak up the extra capacity. **Clearly the wrong choice here** — it's priced for people doing Tokyo↔Kyoto↔Hiroshima-scale trips, not a single one-way northern transect.
- **Nikko Pass (either tier)** — cheap (¥2,040-4,780) but structurally cannot replace the Tobu Nikko-Asakusa limited express fare (surcharge excluded) and is irrelevant to everything before Nikko. Only worth adding *on top of* another ticket if you plan extensive Nikko-area bus-hopping beyond the fixed Chuzenji up/down described here — for exactly two bus rides (Nikko→Chuzenji, Chuzenji→Nikko, ¥2,500 combined) it is not worth ¥4,780 the base fare alone. **Skip it** for this fixed itinerary; pay the two bus fares directly (¥2,500 total) or reserved-seat SPACIA X + pay-as-you-go buses.

### 2.3 Definitive recommendation

**Buy the JR East-South Hokkaido Rail Pass (¥40,000/person)** — it is the only single product that covers the entire rail spine of this itinerary (Sapporo→Hakodate→Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai→Utsunomiya→Nikko, AND round-trips CTS-Sapporo and Sapporo-Otaru at the front, AND the Tobu Nikko-Asakusa limited express reserved seat at the back) for **¥40,000 against a cash-equivalent of ¥44,890-46,530**, a real saving of roughly **¥4,900-6,500 per person (~€26-35)**.

The JR Tohoku-South Hokkaido Pass (¥32,000) is marginally cheaper but does **not** reach the CTS→Sapporo leg, the Otaru round trip, or the Tobu Nikko-Asakusa train — you'd still be paying ¥1,150-1,570 + ¥3,600 + ¥3,050-3,940 = ¥7,800-9,110 out of pocket on top, making the **all-in total for that combination (¥32,000 + ¥7,800-9,110 = ¥39,800-41,110) essentially a wash against the East-South Hokkaido Pass's flat ¥40,000** — same price, more hassle (two separate purchases, two exchange processes), no meaningful advantage. **Go with the single East-South Hokkaido Pass for simplicity at effectively the same cost.**

The saving over pure point-to-point (~¥5,000-6,500/person, ~€27-35/person, ~€54-70 for the couple) is real but modest — not transformative. If you'd rather avoid seat-reservation-machine queues, exchange-order logistics, and the "must be picked up in Japan" step, **paying point-to-point ticket-by-ticket is a legitimate and only slightly more expensive alternative**, and gives you more flexibility to book seats on TokyoWideNikko or SPACIA X directly online without navigating a pass-holder's separate reservation workflow. Given a modest saving and non-trivial extra logistics, this is a genuine toss-up — see recommendation options below.

## 3. The coverage-boundary trap — confirmed

**Yes, this is real, and it must be flagged plainly:** the Sapporo→Hakodate leg (pure JR Hokkaido territory, limited express Hokuto, does NOT touch the Shinkansen network at all) and the Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai leg (Hokkaido Shinkansen, jointly built/operated with JR East/JR Hokkaido coordination) sit on **opposite sides of a real coverage boundary**:

- The **JR Hokkaido Rail Pass** covers Sapporo→Hakodate but explicitly, by name, in its own terms ("The rail pass cannot be used for the Hokkaido Shinkansen"), excludes the onward Shinkansen leg to Sendai.
- No JR Hokkaido-only product covers the Shinkansen leg at all — you must step up to a JR East-affiliated cross-boundary product (Tohoku-South Hokkaido, East-South Hokkaido, or the Nationwide Pass) to get both sides in one pass.

**The two JR East-affiliated cross-boundary passes (Tohoku-South Hokkaido ¥32,000, East-South Hokkaido ¥40,000) are specifically designed to solve exactly this problem** — both explicitly state their area includes "southern Hokkaido (as far as Sapporo)" AND the Hokkaido/Tohoku Shinkansen. So the trap is real for the cheap Hokkaido-only pass, but solved by stepping up one tier. **Do not buy the plain JR Hokkaido Rail Pass and expect it to get you to Sendai — it will not.**

## 6. Verification of the three flagged uncertain items

1. **Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto→Sendai Shinkansen journey time and fare — VERIFIED, and the ~2h30 estimate was too optimistic on the low end / roughly right on average.** Multiple independent sources ([ekitan.com](https://www.ekitan.com/en/planner/shinkansen/section/shin-hakodate-hokuto/sendai/hayabusa), [kkday](https://www.kkday.com/en-us/transportation/japan-rail/shinkansen/jp-shin-hakodate-hokuto-rail-to-jp-sendai-miyagi-rail)) show a range from **2h27m (fastest recorded run) to 2h54m (typical/most commonly quoted), averaging ~2h45m**, with some specific train pairings running as long as 3h21m depending on stops. **Budget 2h45-3h**, not 2h30 flat, and check the specific Hayabusa departure you book — times vary by train number. Fare is consistently **¥18,300 reserved seat** (occasionally quoted ¥18,500 depending on exact date/season pricing).

2. **Otaru Tenguyama Ropeway autumn maintenance closure window for 2026 — VERIFIED, and it does NOT conflict with your 25 Oct visit.** The ropeway's [official 2026 operating-period page](https://tenguyama.ckk.chuo-bus.co.jp/en/hello-world/) states the season runs **25 April 2026 (Sat) through 3 November 2026 (Tue/holiday)** — i.e., no closure gap at all in October; the "closed in spring and autumn" language on some secondary sites refers to the *off-season* shutdown between early November and late April, not a mid-season maintenance window. Your 25 Oct visit falls comfortably within the open season. No risk.

3. **Onuma Quasi-National Park sightseeing boat season end date for 2026 — VERIFIED, resolving the earlier contradiction.** The official Hokkaido tourism listing for "Onuma Yusen" (Onuma Godo Yusen, the boat operator) states the boats are **"Open all year round (closed in case of bad weather)"** with operating hours 9:00-16:20 in summer season and 8:30-15:30 in winter season — i.e., this is a year-round, weather-permitting operation, NOT a seasonal April-October service as one secondary source (activityjapan.com) claimed. The activityjapan.com "April 10-October" figure appears to describe a *specific tour package*, not the boat service itself. **Your 27-28 Oct Hakodate dates are not at risk** if you choose to add an Onuma side trip — the boats run in October under normal winter-transition hours (check exact hours closer to the date, as the summer/winter hour split date itself wasn't pinned down by these sources).
