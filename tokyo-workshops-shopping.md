# Tokyo Workshops & Shopping — Research (4–10 Nov 2026)

## Verdict

Tokyo offers genuinely excellent craft workshops accessible in English — the standouts are indigo dyeing (Wanariya, ¥2,500–8,000), kintsugi (Tsugu Tsugu Ebisu, ¥22,000 with real gold), Edo kiriko glass cutting (Sokichi, ¥3,630), and ramen-from-scratch (Ramen Cooking Tokyo, ¥20,000 with sake+sushi). For shopping, Kappabashi knives + Kuramae craft + Shimokitazawa vintage is a perfect east-to-west day route. **⚠️ CRITICAL:** Japan's tax-free system changes to airport-refund on 1 Nov 2026 — you pay full price in-store and claim the 10% back at departure. Budget for the upfront cost.

**Notes on food workshops:**
- Ramen Cooking Tokyo is the best value date-night option (includes sake, beer, ramen + sushi + photos). Book 1–2 weeks ahead.
- Kamata knife sharpening is the gold standard but requires an interpreter — ⚠️ VERIFY if they allow English-speaking guests with a translation app, or hire a freelance interpreter (~¥5,000/h from gengo.com).
- No true whisky **blending** workshop exists in Tokyo. Tastings only. Suntory Yamazaki (Osaka) offers distillery tours but no personal blending.
- Miso workshop note: you take home ~700g raw miso that ferments 3–6 months. Practical only if you'll actually ferment it at home.

## Part 2 — Shopping

### ⚠️ CRITICAL: Tax-Free System Change (Effective 1 Nov 2026)

**You arrive 3 days AFTER the new system starts.** The old instant-exemption is gone [26].

| Old System (until 31 Oct 2026) | New System (from 1 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|
| Pay tax-exempt at point of sale | Pay FULL price including 10% tax |
| Show passport, sign form | Keep receipts, claim at airport customs terminal |
| Consumables sealed in bag | No sealed bag required |
| Separate consumable/general categories | Single simplified category |

**Action items:**
1. Register on **Visit Japan Web** (vjw.digital.go.jp) BEFORE arrival
2. Get **passport stamped** at immigration (don't use only automated gates)
3. Keep ALL receipts — items must be present at departure
4. Budget **10% more upfront** — refund arrives later
5. Allow **30+ min extra at airport** for customs terminal queue

### Knife Buying Guide (Kappabashi)

| Shop | Est. | Specialty | EN Staff | Tip |
|------|------|-----------|----------|-----|
| **Tsubaya** | 1956 | Widest selection (1,000+) | Yes | Color stickers: yellow=carbon, green=stainless. Handle freely. Start here. |
| **Kamata Hakensha** | 1923 | Every knife hand-sharpened | Yes | 4th-gen master. Also offers sharpening class (¥5,000). 10% discount card with class. |
| **Kamaasa** | — | Whetstones + cookware | Yes | Best whetstone education. Beautiful store. |

Budget recommendation: a good stainless gyuto 210mm for ¥12,000–18,000 is a transformative kitchen upgrade. Carbon steel is cheaper but requires maintenance.

### Vintage Clothing — Size Strategy

⚠️ Japanese domestic brands top out at L ≈ EU M. For EU L/XL:
- Target **American vintage** shops: military surplus, workwear, college hoodies
- Ask for "ōkii saizu" (大きいサイズ)
- **Shimokitazawa:** JAM, KINJI (sold by weight — good for oversized)
- **Koenji:** Safari (military specialist)
- **Harajuku:** Chicago (Omotesando — large American vintage stock)

### Route Efficiency (Suggested Shopping Days)

**East Day:** Kappabashi (knives, kitchen) → Kuramae (craft/homeware) → Akihabara (electronics) — all on/near Asakusa Line

**West Day:** Shimokitazawa (vintage) → Shinjuku: Map Camera (used cameras) + Sekaido (stationery) + Isetan B1 (depachika souvenirs)

**Central Half-Day:** Ginza (Itoya) → Nihonbashi (Ippodo tea, Ozu Washi) → Tokyo Station (Daimaru B1 for last-day souvenirs)

### Flight & Luggage Practicalities

| Item | Rule | Solution |
|------|------|----------|
| **Knives** | MUST be in checked luggage (¥500,000 fine for carry-on attempt) | Wrap in cardboard, center of suitcase |
| **Sake/liquids** | Checked only if >100ml. 24–70% ABV max 5L/person | 720ml bottle = 1.2kg + glass breakage risk. Zip-lock bag wrap. Max 2 bottles. |
| **2 full suitcases already** | 23kg × 2 limit on most EU-bound airlines | Options: (1) Japan Post EMS box 5kg to EU ≈ ¥7,000–9,000 [3–7 days], (2) Buy ¥1,000 duffle at Don Quijote + pay €50–100 extra bag fee, (3) Wear bulky purchases, (4) Compression bags ¥300 at Daiso |
| **Fragile ceramics** | Risk of breakage in hold | Ship via Japan Post or buy from shops that pack for intl shipping |

## Top 3 Workshop Recommendations

1. **Wanariya Indigo Dyeing** (Iriya) — ¥2,500–8,000/pp, genuine family workshop, full English, excellent value, 60–90 min, near great Ueno izakaya. The best craft-to-cost ratio.

2. **Ramen Cooking Tokyo** (Tsukishima) — ¥20,000/pp, 2.5–3h of ramen + sushi + sake. Expensive but it's a full evening experience (date-night quality). Walk to Ginza/Tsukiji for drinks after.

3. **Sokichi Edo Kiriko** (Asakusa) — ¥3,630/pp, 90 min of real diamond-wheel glass cutting. Take home a beautiful cut-glass piece same day. Asakusa is great for dinner. Budget-friendly, genuinely skill-based, unique to Japan.

*Honourable mention: Tsugu Tsugu Kintsugi (Ebisu, ¥22,000) if budget allows — real 23K gold, real urushi, and Ebisu's izakaya street is unbeatable for dinner.*

## Top 3 Shopping Priorities

1. **Kappabashi** (knives + kitchenware) — nowhere else in the world has this density of knife shops at these prices. A ¥15,000 gyuto here equals a €200 knife in Europe. Combine with Kuramae (10 min walk) for craft/homeware.

2. **Map Camera, Shinjuku** — if you use cameras at all, the used gear prices are 20–40% below European equivalents with rigorous quality grading. Worth checking even if you don't plan to buy.

3. **Shimokitazawa** (vintage) — walkable, fun, café-filled, and you'll find unique pieces. Good for a half-day combined with Sekaido (Shinjuku, 1 stop away) for stationery.
