## The two onsen nights — why they work

This is the constraint that has governed every accommodation decision on the trip: **a locked door means the tattoo question never comes up.**

**Casual Euro, Nikko.** The hotel's own site states **全客室 露天風呂付** — *every* room has an open-air bath, and it is the **only** bath in the room. There is **no public bath anywhere** on the property. So the ¥11,000 category is exactly as private as the ¥19,080 Standard Twin, and saves **€87** across two nights.
⚠️ It showed **"1 room left"** when checked — the scarcest inventory on the trip.

**Noboribetsu Grand Hotel.** Private bath is a **single reservable kashikiri unit**, ¥4,400 / 50 min. Reserve it the moment you arrive. If it's taken there is **no fallback**.

### Upgrade path for Noboribetsu

All three alternatives are researched and all have a private bath.

| | All-in | Trade |
|---|---|---|
| **Sekisuitei お任せ** | €177 | **+€22 buys five kashikiri baths** (¥3,300, 15:00–23:00) instead of one. Hotel picks your room — could be small or smoking |
| Sekisuitei standard 和室 | €212 | +€57, specified room, no smoking risk |
| **Takinoya Bekkan Tamanoyu** | €217 | +€62, **outdoor** sulphur kashikiri, intimate 24-room ryokan. No in-room bath — 50 min is your only soak |

⚠️ **Sekisuitei quotes PER PERSON** — the correction that reshaped this section — and **has no room-only rate**; half-board is enforced on every platform. Its dinner is a **buffet, not kaiseki**. Noboribetsu Onsen has only five or six restaurants total, so half-board is right regardless.

⚠️ **VERIFY before booking Sekisuitei:** that the kashikiri baths are **locked rooms, not curtained**. Five independent rooms strongly implies locked, but the whole tattoo strategy rests on it.

**Rejected:** Lake Toya — budget hotels there are communal-bath only, and the one property with a private bath costs *more* plus a 26-minute bus each way. Turtle Inn Nikko — cheaper but 3 km out. Hotel Mahoroba — cheaper still and reportedly tattoo-friendly, but that policy comes from a third-party site, which reverses the private-bath principle.

## The Tokyo week — Tokyu Stay Monzen-Nakacho

### Why an apart-hotel and not an Airbnb

| | 7 nights | Kitchen | Laundry | Front desk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Toyoko Inn class | €372–481 | ✗ | ✗ | ✅ |
| Airbnb (e.g. Stylewood Sangenjaya) | €394–569 | ✅ | washer, **no dryer** | ✗ |
| **Tokyu Stay Monzen-Nakacho** | **€376–788** | ✅ | **washer-dryer in room** | ✅ |
| MIMARU / MONday | €613–1,094 | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**You arrive on day 12 of an 18-night trip, so laundry is the point, not a luxury.** An Airbnb washer with no dryer means three to four hours of bathroom-ventilation drying. A front desk also means bag storage on the midday arrival from Nikko, before the 15:00 check-in.

⚠️ **The minpaku trap, if you reconsider an Airbnb.** **M-prefix** listings are capped at 180 nights a year **and** subject to ward weekday bans — **Shinjuku bans minpaku Monday to Friday** in residential zones, and your week is Wed→Tue, almost all weekdays. That profile is exactly what gets cancelled on arrival. **Non-M listings under the hotel licence (旅館業法) have no such limits**; every apart-hotel chain holds that licence. June 2026 also gave wards power to ban minpaku outright. If you do want an Airbnb, the permissive wards are **Koto, Sumida and Taito** — the same east side as the workshops — and you want a "Licensed Vacation Rental" badge with a non-M number.

## ⚠️ OPEN DECISION: the two Halloween nights

Right now **31 Oct and 1 Nov are an assumed east Shinjuku hotel** at ¥18,000–26,000 and ¥12,000–18,000 — inflated by a Halloween-Saturday Kabukicho premium. It's a placeholder, not a quote.

**East Shinjuku is the researched choice for the *area*** — 5 min to Don Quijote, 5–10 to Ni-chome, so you change at the hotel and walk out in costume; and the first Oedo Line from Roppongi lands you back around 05:05. **The alternative: one booking at Monzen-Nakacho covering all nine Tokyo nights.** The Oedo line reaches both Shinjuku and Roppongi directly, so:

- **31 Oct** — Tokyo Station 11:04 → Shinjuku, Don Quijote by noon → Oedo line to base, change, out to Ni-chome → Roppongi → **first Oedo train home, direct**
- **2 Nov** — leave for Nikko; **the suitcases stay at the same front desk**
- **4 Nov** — Asakusa → base, collect them, **unpack once**

That removes the cross-city move with two large cases on the 4th, the takkyubin question, and the "will they store bags across a gap" question — same booking.

**Cost is roughly a wash:** drop €162–237 of assumed Shinjuku nights, add two nights at Monzen-Nakacho.

**The honest trade-off:** you'd **ride the Oedo line in costume** rather than walking out of the door in Kabukicho. On Halloween in Tokyo that's unremarkable, but it is a real difference.

## Domestic flight — Narita to Sapporo, 24 Oct

### Decision: Peach from Narita Terminal 1

~8 flights a day. **Terminal 1 South Wing — a ten-minute free shuttle** from your T2 arrival.
**~¥10,500–15,000 pp**, plus **¥5,000–7,000 for two checked bags** (LCC base fares exclude them).
Target a **14:00–16:00** departure, landing New Chitose around 15:15–16:15.

**24 October is the last day of the summer timetable** — those flights run on the already-published schedule, so no changeover risk.

Then **JR Rapid Airport → Sapporo, 35 min, ¥1,150 pp — cash**, since it falls outside the pass window.

### Why not JAL, despite Iberia Plus

**JAL does not operate Narita → New Chitose.** Verified — their entire Sapporo service is from **Haneda**, about 20 flights a day.

As an **Iberia Plus** member, **only JAL metal earns you Avios**; Peach and Jetstar earn nothing in that programme. So earning requires a Haneda detour.

| | Total pp | Bags | Arrive Sapporo | Miles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Peach, direct from Narita T1** | ~¥10,500–15,000 | ✗ +¥5,000–7,000 for two | ~15:15–16:15 | ✗ none |
| JAL via Haneda, Explorer Pass | ~¥14,600 *(¥11,000 + ¥3,600 bus)* | ✅ 2×23 kg included | ~16:15–16:45 | ⚠️ unverified |

The **JAL Japan Explorer Pass is ¥11,000 flat with two 23 kg bags included**, open to anyone holding an international ticket on any airline — your Iberia booking reference qualifies. **So price is roughly a wash.**

**What the detour costs is ~90 minutes and a forfeit risk.** The Limousine Bus is 75–85 minutes plus road traffic, and no airline sells a through-ticket across a ground transfer between two airports — so the domestic leg is a separate ticket either way, and **if IB281 runs late that ticket is lost.** Two failure points instead of one, on a day you've already flown fourteen hours.

**And the payoff is small even if it works.** Haneda–New Chitose is only about **510 miles** — a few hundred Avios at full accrual, rounding error against a Madrid–Tokyo round trip. ⚠️ **Whether Explorer Pass fares earn Avios at all is unverified**; deeply discounted promotional fares often earn reduced or zero miles. Check the fare rules on jal.co.jp if you want to overrule this.

**Also considered:** **ANA from Narita** runs only 1–2 daily on NRT–CTS and a departure in the window couldn't be confirmed — possibly seasonal. **Jetstar Japan from Terminal 3** (~15 min from T2) is comparable in price and **earns JAL Mileage Bank miles directly** — the best answer if you held JMB rather than Iberia Plus, since it earns with no airport transfer. Jetstar does **not** credit to Iberia Plus.

### Why not the cheaper ¥32,000 Tohoku–South Hokkaido pass

Its area is defined prefecture by prefecture — Fukushima, Miyagi, Yamagata, Akita, Iwate, Aomori, plus southern Hokkaido. **No Kanto, no Tokyo.** Sendai→Tokyo is covered only as far as Fukushima Prefecture, so you'd pay the out-of-area stretch south of there at the gate — landing within a few hundred yen of the ¥40,000 pass.

**A dead heat on price, so take the ¥40,000 one on simplicity:** no boundary fare adjustment, free reservations throughout, Tokyo local lines included.

### Buying and collecting

**Buy mid-August to late September**, on **JR East's own site**:
- Before the **1 October price revision**
- An exchange order is **valid three months from issue**, so an August purchase still covers a 26 October activation

**Collect at a passport-reader machine at Narita**, T1 or T2, on arrival morning — budget it into the 2.5–3.5 h you're already allowing.

**Seat reservations open one month ahead.** The two Hokuto legs are mandatory, not optional — that train has **no unreserved cars at all**.

## ⚠️ Luggage — oversized baggage is mandatory

You have **two large suitcases plus two small bags**.

Bags between **160 cm and 250 cm combined dimensions** need a **free reservation made at the time of ticket purchase** on the Shinkansen. It's **¥1,000 per bag** if you board without one. Most 28–30 inch hardsides sit right at that threshold; anything under 160 cm is exempt.

**Affects two legs:**
- **29 Oct** — Hayabusa 18, Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto → Sendai
- **31 Oct** — Hayabusa, Sendai → Tokyo

**Not** the Hokuto legs (proper racks, no size rule) and **not** the Tobu express to Nikko.

Don't plan on coin lockers as a workaround — even "large" ones are about 86 × 34 × 65 cm.

**The Nikko gap:** both large cases stay at the Tokyo hotel across 2–3 Nov; you take an overnight bag. Confirm storage in the booking — see `ACCOMMODATION.md`.

**Knives** bought in Kappabashi must go in **checked** baggage — ¥500,000 fine for attempting carry-on. **Sake** is checked only, max 5 L pp at 24–70% ABV; a 720 ml bottle is 1.2 kg plus breakage risk, so cap it at two. If you overbuy, **Japan Post EMS is ¥7,000–9,000 for 5 kg to Europe** — cheaper and safer than an extra airline bag.

## 1. RENTAL IS NOT AN OPTION — you buy

**Same-day walk-in Halloween costume rental for adults does not exist in Tokyo.** Verified across
English and Japanese sources. Tokyo's Halloween culture is purchase-based: adults buy cheap
costumes or improvise them.

What the "rental" market actually is, and why none of it works:

| Investigated | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Tokyo Cosplay (hotel delivery) | Japanese cultural costumes only, sizes S–L Japanese. No Halloween range |
| ME TOKYO, Shibuya | Indoor photo experience — **you cannot leave in the costume** |
| DMM いろいろレンタル | Mail-order, 3–7 day lead time, likely discontinued |
| Ikebukuro Cosplay Festival | Adult rental: none. Children only |
| ACOS (Animate Cosplay) | Sells, does not rent |
| Studio Alice | Children's photo studio |

**So: buy on the afternoon of the 31st.** That is also your first and only opportunity — you're in
Tohoku until that morning.

## 2. THE SHOPPING ROUTE

### Sizing is the real problem, and there's a clean answer

Japanese S/M will be picked over by afternoon. **Your advantage: LL/XL stock is far less
contested**, because Japanese customers overwhelmingly buy S/M.

Safest fits for European bodies:
- **Kigurumi onesies** — "free size" fits to ~180 cm, XL versions to 186 cm. Always deep stock
- **Inflatable costumes** — one-size by design (T-Rex, sumo). ¥3,000–5,000
- **Zentai / morphsuits** — stretch fabric, very forgiving
- **DIY** — black Uniqlo base plus Daiso accessories. Guaranteed fit

**Get to Don Quijote by noon, not 15:00.** Those three hours materially change selection.

## 5. WARNINGS — read these

### Street drinking is banned where you'll be

| Ward | Scope | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Shinjuku** | Kabukicho 1-chome + Shinjuku 3-chome | **17:00 on 31 Oct → 05:00 on 1 Nov** |
| **Shibuya** | All streets and parks near the station | 18:00–05:00, daily, year-round |
| **Minato (Roppongi)** | No ban | — |

No fines, but confiscation and verbal enforcement. **Drink inside venues.** Konbini in Shibuya and
Shinjuku may also be told to stop selling alcohol during restricted hours.

### Change at the hotel

There are **no temporary changing spaces** — the wards are discouraging costume gatherings, not
facilitating them. Station toilets are crowded and staff discourage lingering. A **manga cafe**
booth (¥500–1,000/hour) is the only decent backup, and they fill up on Halloween.

### Riding the train in costume

Not banned, but **strongly discouraged if gory.** Keio Railway formally asks passengers to refrain
from costumes that make others uneasy. Anime, pop culture and kigurumi are fine. Fake blood and
horror masks will draw stares and possibly staff intervention.

### Safety — the two that matter

**Roppongi touts.** Still active and confirmed through 2025, concentrated on Gaien-Higashi Dori,
and **extra aggressive on Halloween.** The pattern is: lure into a bar → inflated bill of
¥30,000–100,000+ → intimidation. **Never follow a tout.** Say no and keep walking. Pick your
venues in advance. If you're trapped, insist on calling the police on **110**.

**Drink spiking.** Roppongi and Kabukicho are the documented hotspots — spiked drink,
unconsciousness, credit-card fraud. Never leave a drink unattended. If you feel suddenly and
extremely drunk after one or two drinks, leave immediately.

### Passport

**You are legally required to carry it at all times** — fine up to ¥100,000, and police ID checks
do happen on Halloween. Carry it in a pouch under the costume, and leave a photocopy at the hotel.
Do not put it in a club cloakroom.

### Smaller things

Coin lockers at Shibuya and Shinjuku will be **full** — try one station out (Yoyogi, Shin-Okubo).
**Ecbo Cloak** is app-based bag storage at shops, ¥400–700, reserve ahead. Ask before photographing
people: *shashin ii desu ka?* And Tokyo residential streets are library-quiet at 05:00 — keep it
down walking back.

**Emergency:** Police **110** · Ambulance/Fire **119** · JNTO tourist hotline (24/7 English)
**050-3816-2787** · koban police boxes are manned around the clock at major intersections.
