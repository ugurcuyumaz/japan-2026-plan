# Practicalities

The things that aren't itinerary, transport or money, and that the plan was silent on until
**Most of this has to be sorted before you fly.**

---

## 1. Connectivity — ⚠️ nothing was planned, and you need it

**Get an eSIM before departure. Ubigi is the current pick** — NTT Docomo + KDDI, 5G, roughly
**€15–27 for the trip**. Install the profile at home; activate on landing.

This is not a comfort item. You need data for:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The GO taxi app** | Halloween night has a **00:30–05:00 rail blackout**. Shape B is built to walk home from Kabukicho, so this is the backup rather than the plan — worth having if you end up further out than Shinjuku |
| **Live train checks** | The Hokuto services on 26 and 27 Oct are **all-reserved with no unreserved cars** — a delay cascades, and you need to know |
| **Maps** | Nikko and Noboribetsu are the two places you're least likely to find your way by eye |
| **Earthquake alerts** | See §6 |

## 2. IC card — the 2019 answer no longer works

**Regular Suica and PASMO are still suspended for tourist purchase.** You cannot walk up and buy
one on arrival any more. Two working options:

- **Apple Wallet Suica** — works on any **iPhone XS or newer regardless of where it was bought**,
  tops up by credit card in-app. Set it up before you fly. This is the easy answer.
- **Welcome Suica** or **Tourist PASMO** — physical cards, available at the airport, **28-day
  validity**, no deposit.

You'll want one: the plan leans on cash-only local buses in Noboribetsu and Nikko, JR local
lines, the Enoden, four Tokyo drinking districts and konbini.

---

## 3. Cash — how much, and where

Japan is much more card-friendly than in 2019, but this itinerary specifically is not.
**Cash-only or cash-preferred on your route:** Toshogu (¥1,600–2,400 pp), the Monzen-Nakacho
standing izakayas, Ni-chome bars, the JR Rapid Airport on the 24th, every local bus, and the
workshops.

**Arrive with ¥30,000–50,000.** Buy yen at a Spanish forex bureau before you fly — rates for JPY
are generally better pre-trip than at Narita.

**Refill at Seven Bank ATMs** inside any 7-Eleven — 24 h, all Visa and Mastercard, ¥100,000 per
transaction. Japan Post ATMs are the backup.

**Rough daily cash:** ¥10,000–15,000 in Nikko and Noboribetsu · ¥5,000–8,000 in Tokyo.

## 4. Power

**Spain is Type C/F at 230 V. Japan is Type A at 100 V.**

- **Phones, laptops, camera chargers** are all 100–240 V — they need only a **€3 plug adapter**.
  Buy two or three before you fly; Japanese shops don't stock Euro-to-Japan adapters.
- **Hair dryers and straighteners will run at roughly half power.** Every hotel on this trip
  provides a dryer. Leave yours at home.

## 5. Medication — ⚠️ the one with real consequences

**Codeine is banned outright in Japan** as a narcotic requiring an import permit. So is
**pseudoephedrine**.

⚠️ **Two common Spanish cold remedies contain codeine: Frenadol and Codeisan.** Sudafed is
pseudoephedrine. Do not pack any of them out of habit — this is a customs matter, not an
inconvenience.

**Prescriptions** under one month's supply need no *Yakkan Shoumei* import certificate, **but must
travel in original packaging with your name on it**. Check anything you're unsure about against
the MHLW list: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/english/policy/health-medical/pharmaceuticals/

## 6. Earthquakes

Late October to November is low seismic season, but install **"Safety Tips"** (Japan Tourism
Agency, free, English and Spanish push alerts) and **NHK World** before you fly.

**If it happens:** in a hotel, get under a desk or in a doorframe, **never the lift** · on a
Shinkansen, brace — the trains auto-brake · on the coast after strong shaking, **move uphill
immediately** and don't wait for an announcement.

## 7. Clothing — a 14-degree swing inside one week

The plan never said what to pack, and "Japan in late October" is not one climate here.

| Where | When | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Hokkaido** — Sapporo, Noboribetsu, Hakodate | 24–28 Oct | **4–14 °C** |
| **Sendai** | 29–30 Oct | 8–17 °C |
| **Nikko** | 2–3 Nov | **3–13 °C** — the coldest mornings of the trip |
| **Tokyo** | 31 Oct – 10 Nov | 10–18 °C |

**Pack in layers.** A proper autumn jacket for Hokkaido, not a hoodie. A fleece or down liner plus
a windproof shell for the **08:20 Nikko taxi on 3 Nov**, which is the single coldest moment on the
itinerary. Light jacket weather in Tokyo. Comfortable shoes with grip for Motomachi's cobbled
slopes and the Nikko precinct.

The **in-room washer-dryer** at Monzen-Nakacho is why you can pack light for eighteen nights.

## 8. Arrival checklist, in order

1. ⚠️ **Get the passport physically stamped.** The booth is **after** the automated biometric
   gates, signed **スタンプコーナー / Stamp Corner**. Sail through the e-gates without doubling
   back and **you cannot do tax-free shopping for the entire trip.**
2. Activate the eSIM.
3. Collect the JR pass at the passport-reader machine, T1 or T2.
4. Withdraw or confirm cash.
5. T2 → T1 free shuttle, 10 min, for the Peach flight.

Tax-free mechanics and the 1 November change: `TRANSPORT.md`.
