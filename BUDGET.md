## Shopping — deliberately unbudgeted

Entirely discretionary, so it sits outside the total. For reference: a good **gyuto knife ¥12,000–18,000** · **used camera gear ¥30,000–300,000** at Map Camera · **vintage clothing ¥500–15,000** a piece · **depachika food gifts ¥500–5,000** an item.

⚠️ **From 1 November 2026 you pay the full 10% tax in-store** and reclaim at Narita customs — so **budget 10% more cash up front** for everything bought in the Tokyo week. Register on Visit Japan Web first, get the passport stamped on arrival, keep every receipt.

**If you buy heavy:** a Japan Post EMS box home is **¥7,000–9,000 for 5 kg**, cheaper and safer than an extra airline bag.

## Biggest cost levers, in order

1. **The Tokyo block's 8 nights** — a €473 spread, and the only line where a real quote could move the total materially.
2. **Food across 18 days** — a **€492 spread** combined (Leg 1 €433–753, Leg 2 €237–409). Markets, ramen and standing izakaya at the bottom; restaurant dinners at the top. Monzen-Nakacho's izakaya at ¥3,000–6,000 an evening is already the cheap end.
3. **The NRT–CTS flight** — moves with lead time. Book mid-Aug to late Sep.
4. **The rail pass** — fixed at €430, and it only saves **~€39** for the two of you. See `TRANSPORT.md`.
5. **Workshops** — the two picks are **€63–98** together, now that Wanariya's per-item prices are known. The ramen (€215) and gold-kintsugi (€237) sessions would each cost more than a whole onsen night.
6. **Food at the low end still assumes konbini lunches.** The Leg 2 floor was raised from ¥5,000 to ¥5,500 a day for two because ¥5,000 was not credible against a single ¥3,000–6,000 izakaya evening. The bottom of the band is real but disciplined.
