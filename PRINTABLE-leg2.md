## Thu 5 Nov — KAMAKURA + ENOSHIMA ⭐ *(day trip)*

A one-way westward run with **no backtracking**: JR out to Kamakura, the **Enoden** coastal tram down to Enoshima, then home from Fujisawa on a different line.

| | |
|---|---|
| 08:30 | Monzen-Nakacho → **Tokyo Station**, 5 min |
| ~09:00 | **JR Yokosuka Line → Kamakura**, ~55 min |
| 10:00–11:00 | **Komachi-dori** street food, then **Tsurugaoka Hachimangu** at the top of it — the Minamoto shrine, free |
| 11:15 | **ENODEN → Hase** (2 stops). Buy the **Nori-ori-kun 1-day Enoden pass** if you're hopping |
| 11:30–13:00 | **KOTOKU-IN — the Great Buddha** ⭐, 11.4 m of bronze cast in 1252, sitting in the open air. Then **HASE-DERA** next door — hillside temple with a sea-view terrace |
| 13:00 | **Lunch: shirasu-don** (しらす丼) — Sagami Bay whitebait, the local dish. **Raw *nama-shirasu* depends on the boats going out**, so ask; boiled is always available |
| 14:15 | **ENODEN → Enoshima**, along the coast |
| 14:45–16:45 | **ENOSHIMA** — the bridge, **Enoshima Shrine**, the clifftop paths, the **Sea Candle** observation tower. **Sunset ~16:40**, and on a clear early-November evening **Mount Fuji is visible across the bay** |
| ~17:15 | Walk/tram to **Fujisawa**, **JR to Tokyo Station**, home by ~19:00 |

**~¥14,400–17,400 for the two of you** — trains, entries, food. About **€77–94.**

⚠️ **Fares, opening hours and entry prices here are from general knowledge, not a verified source.** Ballpark per person: JR Tokyo–Kamakura ~¥950 each way · Enoden day pass ~¥800 · Kotoku-in ¥300 · Hase-dera ¥400 · Enoshima Shrine free · Sea Candle ~¥500 · the Escar escalator ~¥360. **Confirm before you go** — and skipping the Sea Candle and Escar brings the day down to roughly €54–75.

**Alternative if you'd rather have quiet than the shrine:** **Hokoku-ji**, the bamboo-grove temple with matcha served in the grove, is east of Kamakura station and needs a short bus. Swap it for Tsurugaoka Hachimangu rather than adding it.

**Why Thursday works:** a weekday in November means the Enoden — which is genuinely packed at weekends and in hydrangea season — is manageable, and you get the coast to yourselves.

## Fri 6 Nov — EAST DAY: knives, craft, glass cutting

Everything today is within about twenty minutes' walk on the east side, and it's all a short hop from your door.

| | |
|---|---|
| Morning | **KAPPABASHI (合羽橋)** — the kitchenware street. **Tsubaya** for selection (1,000+ knives, yellow sticker = carbon, green = stainless, handle them freely), **Kamata Hakensha** for hand-sharpened, **Kamaasa** for whetstones |
| | A good **stainless gyuto 210 mm at ¥12,000–18,000** is the buy. ⚠️ **Knives go in CHECKED baggage** — ¥500,000 fine for attempting carry-on |
| Midday | **KURAMAE (蔵前)** — 10 min walk. **Kakimori** custom notebooks · **Maito** plant-dyed textiles · **Koncent** design goods |
| Afternoon | **SOKICHI — EDO KIRIKO glass cutting, Asakusa.** ¥3,630 pp, 90–120 min, 30 seconds from Exit 4. Real diamond-wheel cutting; you take the piece home the same day |
| Evening | **JIMBOCHO / KANDA** — 10 min from base. The strongest せんべろ (sen-bero) scene in the city: **Stand Hero**, **Atsumori**, **Ichigō**. ¥1,000 sets exist |

⚠️ **Jimbocho on a weekday, not the weekend.** These are salaryman bars — many close Saturday and Sunday. Friday is your best window.

⚠️ **The tax-free system changed on 1 November 2026**, three days before this week starts. You **paid the full 10% in-store** all week and claim it back **at the airport**, so: **register on Visit Japan Web before you fly out to Japan**, get your **passport physically stamped at immigration on arrival** (don't use only the automated gates), **keep every receipt**, and have the goods with you at departure. This is why the airport buffer matters. ⚠️ Re-check in September in case implementation slipped.

**Do not use N'EX** — ¥3,070 pp against ¥1,300 for the bus, and the bus is easier with two large cases.

## Workshop menu — the full set researched

Picked above: **Edo kiriko** (Fri), **indigo** (Mon), plus the **wasabi harvest** (Thu). These are the alternatives if you'd rather swap.

**Craft** — Wanariya indigo ¥2,500–8,000 pp (Iriya) · Mizuno dye ¥1,600–3,000 by weight (Yurakucho, walk-in Sat/Sun) · Sokichi Edo kiriko ¥3,630 (Asakusa) · Uzumako pottery wheel **¥3,960 couple plan** (Akabanebashi) · Ozu Washi papermaking ¥3,500–6,000 (Nihonbashi) · glass-blowing ¥4,400–5,500 (Ota-ku) · Sashiko with a bilingual author ¥5,000–7,000 (Ogikubo, home studio) · furoshiki ¥5,250 (Shirokane) · leather ¥5,500–7,700 (Aoyama) · calligraphy ¥5,000–6,500 (Asakusa) · ikebana ¥6,500–8,000 (Ichigaya) · **kintsugi with real 23K gold and urushi ¥22,000** (Ebisu — or ¥6,600 for the epoxy version)

**Food** — Jyuwari soba ¥8,500 pp, 50 min (Kappabashi — locals attend) · miso making ¥10,000 (Daimon) · sushi ¥15,400–17,600 (Tsukiji) · wagashi nerikiri ¥4,000–8,700 (Sendagi artisan home studio) · sake and wagyu pairing ¥19,500 (Nishi-Azabu) · **ramen from scratch ¥20,000** (Tsukishima — 2.5–3 h with sake and sushi, a full evening) · knife sharpening ¥5,000 (Kappabashi, ⚠️ **Japanese only**) or ¥7,000–10,000 via Airbnb in English

**Other** — tea ceremony ¥3,500 (Shibuya, intimate) · taiko ¥8,000–10,000 · samurai/kendo ¥7,500–15,000 (more photo-op than skill unless you take the full armoured kendo)

**Dead ends:** Mokuhankan no longer runs woodblock print parties (shop only) · no whisky *blending* exists in Tokyo, tastings only · Gundam Base is a free build zone, not a structured workshop.

**On price:** the two picks come to about **€66 for both of you combined**. The ramen and gold-kintsugi sessions are €215 and €237 respectively for two — each more than your entire Noboribetsu onsen night with dinner and a private bath.
