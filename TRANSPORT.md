⚠️ **Confirm whether IB282 is a single leg.** Madrid is UTC+1 by then (DST ended 25 Oct) and a westbound Tokyo–Madrid leg runs 14–15 hours, so a direct lands around **20:00–21:00 the same evening**. **05:45 cannot be a direct arrival** — it would imply a connection with an overnight layover. Worth knowing where and how long before you're 30 hours into a journey.

**Either way you don't gain a night in Japan** — you still leave Narita on the 11th, so 24 Oct to 10 Nov is 18 nights.

## Domestic flight — Narita to Sapporo, 24 Oct

### Decision: Peach from Narita Terminal 1

~8 flights a day. **Terminal 1 South Wing — a ten-minute free shuttle** from your T2 arrival.
**~¥10,500–15,000 pp**, plus **¥7,500–10,000 for checked bags** — Peach charges **¥2,490 per piece per segment** (20 kg, booked online), and the cabin allowance is **7 kg total including your personal item**, so neither large suitcase can go in the cabin. Two large cases alone are ¥4,980; add the two small bags if they won't fit the cabin limit and you are at **3–4 pieces, ¥7,470–9,960.**
Target a **14:00–16:00** departure, landing New Chitose around 15:15–16:15.

**24 October is the last day of the summer timetable** — those flights run on the already-published schedule, so no changeover risk.

Then **JR Rapid Airport → Sapporo, 33–37 min, ¥1,150–1,230 pp — cash**, since it falls outside the pass window. ⚠️ Sources give both ¥1,150 and ¥1,230 — budget the higher.

### Why not JAL, despite Iberia Plus

**JAL does not operate Narita → New Chitose.** Verified — their entire Sapporo service is from **Haneda**, about 20 flights a day.

As an **Iberia Plus** member, **only JAL metal earns you Avios**; Peach and Jetstar earn nothing in that programme. So earning requires a Haneda detour.

| | Total pp | Bags | Arrive Sapporo | Miles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Peach, direct from Narita T1** | ~¥10,500–15,000 | ✗ **+¥7,500–10,000** (¥2,490/piece) | ~15:15–16:15 | ✗ none |
| JAL via Haneda, Explorer Pass | ~¥14,600 *(¥11,000 + ¥3,600 bus)* | ✅ 2×23 kg included | ~16:15–16:45 | ⚠️ unverified |

The **JAL Japan Explorer Pass is ¥11,000 flat with two 23 kg bags included**, open to anyone holding an international ticket on any airline — your Iberia booking reference qualifies. **So price is roughly a wash.**

**What the detour costs is ~90 minutes and a forfeit risk.** The Limousine Bus is 75–85 minutes plus road traffic, and no airline sells a through-ticket across a ground transfer between two airports — so the domestic leg is a separate ticket either way, and **if IB281 runs late that ticket is lost.** Two failure points instead of one, on a day you've already flown fourteen hours.

**And the payoff is small even if it works.** Haneda–New Chitose is only about **510 miles** — a few hundred Avios at full accrual, rounding error against a Madrid–Tokyo round trip. ⚠️ **Whether Explorer Pass fares earn Avios at all is unverified**; deeply discounted promotional fares often earn reduced or zero miles. Check the fare rules on jal.co.jp if you want to overrule this.

**Also considered:** **ANA from Narita** runs only 1–2 daily on NRT–CTS and a departure in the window couldn't be confirmed — possibly seasonal. **Jetstar Japan from Terminal 3** (~15 min from T2) is comparable in price and **earns JAL Mileage Bank miles directly** — the best answer if you held JMB rather than Iberia Plus, since it earns with no airport transfer. Jetstar does **not** credit to Iberia Plus.

## Rail pass

### Buy: JR East–South Hokkaido Rail Pass, ¥40,000 pp. Activate 26 October.

**Six CONSECUTIVE calendar days**, midnight to midnight → **covers 26–31 October.**

⚠️ **There is no flexible-day version.** Reseller listings advertising "6 flexible days within 14" are stale — the flexible product was discontinued in March 2026. Verified against japan-guide, page updated 2026-03-14. Idle days can't be skipped over.

### Why that window

An exact fit for the leg's expensive rail:

| Date | Leg | ¥ pp |
|---|---|---|
| 26 Oct | Hokuto, Sapporo → Noboribetsu | 4,890 |
| 27 Oct | Hokuto, Noboribetsu → Hakodate | 7,790 |
| 29 Oct | Local to Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto | 440 |
| 29 Oct | Hayabusa 18 → Sendai | 18,300 |
| 30 Oct | Senseki Line, Matsushima return | ~840 |
| 31 Oct | Hayabusa, Sendai → Tokyo | 11,630 |

Only the two cheapest legs fall outside: the **¥1,150–1,230 pp airport rapid on the 24th** and the **Narita transfer on 11 Nov**. Both cash.

**Saving: ¥3,670 pp — roughly €39 for the couple.** Thinner than it looks, and worth being clear about: at that margin you are **buying flexibility, not savings**. What you actually get is free unlimited seat reservations on two all-reserved Hokuto services and two Shinkansen, no ticket-machine queues, and JR East local lines in Tokyo on Halloween Saturday. If the itinerary loses even one long leg, the pass goes negative — re-check the sum before you buy in September.

### Why not the cheaper ¥32,000 Tohoku–South Hokkaido pass

Its area is defined prefecture by prefecture — Fukushima, Miyagi, Yamagata, Akita, Iwate, Aomori, plus southern Hokkaido. **No Kanto, no Tokyo.** Sendai→Tokyo is covered only as far as Fukushima Prefecture, so you'd pay the out-of-area stretch south of there at the gate — landing within a few hundred yen of the ¥40,000 pass.

**A dead heat on price, so take the ¥40,000 one on simplicity:** no boundary fare adjustment, free reservations throughout, Tokyo local lines included.

### Buying and collecting

**Buy mid-August to late September**, on **JR East's own site**:
- Before the **1 October price revision**
- An exchange order is **valid three months from issue**, so an August purchase still covers a 26 October activation

**Collect at a passport-reader machine at Narita**, T1 or T2, on arrival morning — budget it into the 2.5–3.5 h you're already allowing.

**Seat reservations open one month ahead.** The two Hokuto legs are mandatory, not optional — that train has **no unreserved cars at all**.

## Nikko is not pass-covered

Not in this window. **Buy the Tobu limited express tickets in cash**, ¥3,050–3,940 pp each way — Revaty at the bottom of that range, SPACIA X at the top. It's the train, not the time of day: Japanese rail fares are regulated with **no time-of-day or demand pricing and no advance-purchase discount**. Booking early buys a seat, not a better price.

*There is also a **direct limited express to Nikko from Shinjuku**, not just from Asakusa. A pass window reaching 2 November could cover it — but only by sacrificing both Hokuto legs (¥12,680) to gain ~¥3,500. About ¥9,000 worse. Rejected.*

## ⚠️ Luggage — oversized baggage is mandatory

You have **two large suitcases plus two small bags**.

Bags of **161–250 cm combined dimensions** need a **free reservation made at the time of ticket purchase** — **on the Shinkansen only.** It's **¥1,000 per bag** if you board without one, and **over 250 cm is refused outright**. Most 28–30 inch hardsides sit right at that threshold; anything **160 cm or under is exempt**.

✅ **It does not apply to conventional limited expresses** — so the **Hokuto** on 26–27 Oct and the **Tobu Spacia** on 2–3 Nov are unaffected. Both have luggage racks.

**Affects two legs:**
- **29 Oct** — Hayabusa 18, Shin-Hakodate-Hokuto → Sendai
- **31 Oct** — Hayabusa, Sendai → Tokyo

**Not** the Hokuto legs (proper racks, no size rule) and **not** the Tobu express to Nikko.

Don't plan on coin lockers as a workaround — even "large" ones are about 86 × 34 × 65 cm.

**The Nikko gap:** both large cases go to Tokyu Stay Monzen-Nakacho on the morning of 2 Nov and wait there across the **single Nikko night**; you take an overnight bag. You collect them when you check in on the evening of 3 Nov. Confirm the day-before drop in the booking — see `ACCOMMODATION.md`.

**Knives** bought in Kappabashi must go in **checked** baggage — ¥500,000 fine for attempting carry-on. **Sake** is checked only, max 5 L pp at 24–70% ABV; a 720 ml bottle is 1.2 kg plus breakage risk, so cap it at two. If you overbuy, **Japan Post EMS is ¥7,000–9,000 for 5 kg to Europe** — cheaper and safer than an extra airline bag.

## Airport transfer home — 11 Nov

**TYO-NRT bus from Tokyo Station Yaesu South Exit, ~75 min, ¥1,500 pp.** Monzen-Nakacho → Tokyo Station is ~10 min on the Tozai Line. **Take the 09:00 or 09:30 departure** for the 14:10 flight.

⚠️ **Not Nihonbashi, and not ¥1,300.** The old *Access Narita* service merged into **TYO-NRT**, which stops at **Tokyo Station and Ginza only**. Source: tyo-nrt.com.

**N'EX is the rail alternative** at ¥3,070 pp — 53 min from Tokyo Station, unaffected by traffic. The bus is cheaper and fine with two large cases, but it runs on the Bayshore expressway: a routine incident costs 30–40 min, a bad one puts you at Narita past 12:30 against a 13:10 check-in. On the last day of the trip, €17 for the two of you buys rails instead of roads.

## ⚠️ Tax-free shopping changed on 1 November 2026

Your trip **straddles the changeover**, which splits your shopping into two regimes.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Through 31 Oct** | Old system — tax deducted at the register, nothing to reclaim |
| **From 1 Nov** | New system — **you pay the full 10% in-store** and claim it back at Narita customs |

**Consequences for the Tokyo week, which is entirely under the new system:**
1. **Register on Visit Japan Web** before you fly out
2. **Get your passport physically stamped at immigration on arrival** — don't use only the automated gates
3. **Keep every receipt**, and have the goods with you at departure
4. **Budget 10% more cash up front**
5. **Allow 30+ minutes extra at Narita** on the 11th

**If you'd buy something either side of the date, buy it before 1 November** and skip the refund queue. Sendai's covered arcades on 29–30 Oct are the practical window; Kappabashi isn't, since you're in Tohoku until the 31st.

⚠️ **Re-check in September** in case implementation slipped — that would revert everything to deduct-at-register.
