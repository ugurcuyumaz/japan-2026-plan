## Why an apart-hotel and not an Airbnb

| | 7 nights | Kitchen | Laundry | Front desk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Toyoko Inn class | €372–481 | ✗ | ✗ | ✅ |
| Airbnb (e.g. Stylewood Sangenjaya) | €394–569 | ✅ | washer, **no dryer** | ✗ |
| **Tokyu Stay Monzen-Nakacho** | **see below** | ✅ | **washer-dryer in room** | ✅ |
| MIMARU / MONday | €613–1,094 | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**You arrive on day 12 of an 18-night trip, so laundry is the point, not a luxury.** An Airbnb washer with no dryer means three to four hours of bathroom-ventilation drying. A front desk also means bag storage on the midday arrival from Nikko, before the 15:00 check-in.

⚠️ **The minpaku trap, if you reconsider an Airbnb.** **M-prefix** listings are capped at 180 nights a year **and** subject to ward weekday bans — **Shinjuku bans minpaku Monday to Friday** in residential zones, and your week is Wed→Tue, almost all weekdays. That profile is exactly what gets cancelled on arrival. **Non-M listings under the hotel licence (旅館業法) have no such limits.** Every apart-hotel chain holds that licence. June 2026 also gave wards power to ban minpaku outright. If you do want an Airbnb, the permissive wards are **Koto, Sumida and Taito** — the same east side as the workshops — and you want a "Licensed Vacation Rental" badge with a non-M number.

## Why Monzen-Nakacho

Two independent research agents picked it — one judging izakaya density, the other judging week-long accommodation. That's the strongest convergence on any decision in this trip.

**Izakaya, which was your stated deciding criterion.** Old shitamachi around Tomioka Hachiman: **Tatsumi Shindō yokocho**, **Uosan Sakaba** (trading since 1949), **Takioka** standing bar, **Orihara Shōten** kakuuchi. **¥3,000–6,000 an evening for two.** Effectively no tourists, and — unlike Jimbocho, the runner-up — **open seven days**.

**Transit, which decides everything else:**

| To | Time |
|---|---|
| **Tokyo Station** — for the Odoriko to Izu | **5 min** |
| **Asakusa** — for the Tobu Nikko line, and the workshops | **11 min** |
| **Shinjuku** — Shimokitazawa, Map Camera | 15 min |
| Jimbocho · Kitasenju | 10 min each |
| Akabane | 20 min |
| **Narita** — Access Narita bus from **Nihonbashi**, ¥1,300 pp | **75 min** |

It's on the **Toei Oedo line**, which also runs direct to **Roppongi** — the reason the Halloween extension below is possible at all.

## Optional: extend to nine nights and absorb Halloween

Currently leg 1 puts 31 Oct and 1 Nov in an **assumed east Shinjuku hotel** at ¥18,000–26,000 and ¥12,000–18,000 — inflated by a Halloween-Saturday Kabukicho premium.

**One booking here instead would cover all nine Tokyo nights**, because the Oedo line reaches both Shinjuku and Roppongi directly:

- **31 Oct** — Tokyo Station 11:04 → Shinjuku, Don Quijote Kabukicho by noon → Oedo line to base, change, out to Ni-chome → Roppongi → **first Oedo train home, direct**
- **2 Nov** — leave for Nikko; **the suitcases stay at the same front desk**
- **4 Nov** — Asakusa → base, collect them, **unpack once**

That removes the cross-city move with two large cases on the 4th, the takkyubin forwarding question, and the "will they store bags across a gap" question — it's the same booking.

**Cost is roughly a wash:** drop €162–237 of assumed Shinjuku nights, add two nights here.

**The trade-off is honest:** you'd **ride the Oedo line in costume** rather than walking out of the door in Kabukicho. On Halloween in Tokyo that's unremarkable, but it is a real difference from the current plan.

⚠️ If you take this, **leg 1 needs updating** — `02-itinerary/PRINTABLE-leg1.md` (31 Oct routes via Shinjuku) and `04-bookings/accommodation-leg1.md` (the two assumed nights).
