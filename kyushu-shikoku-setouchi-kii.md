## ⚠️ Two critical, time-sensitive corrections up front

### 1. There is NO Setouchi Triennale session in autumn 2026
The Triennale is **triennial**, not annual. The most recent edition was **Setouchi Triennale 2025** (spring/summer/autumn sessions across 2025), and the official organizer states the next edition will be **2028**. [setouchi-artfest.jp/en/about/mission-and-history](https://setouchi-artfest.jp/en/about/mission-and-history/) explicitly frames "Setouchi Triennale 2025" as "the sixth triennial," and [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e5410.html) states "the next triennale is expected to take place in 2028." [Setouchi Explorer](https://www.setouchiexplorer.com/setouchi-triennale/) confirms: "The Setouchi Triennale 2025 is over. The next one will be held in 2028." One third-party aggregator page ([en.japantravel.com/kagawa/setouchi-triennale/71172](https://en.japantravel.com/kagawa/setouchi-triennale/71172)) is mislabeled "Setouchi Triennale 2026" but on inspection contains a "dates not yet confirmed" disclaimer and reuses the identical Oct 3–Nov 9 dates that were the actual **2025** autumn session — this is a stale/duplicated listing, not evidence of a 2026 edition. **Conclusion: plan Naoshima/Teshima/Inujima/Shodoshima as a normal (non-festival) art-island visit.** This is good news logistically — no festival crowding, no festival-only ferry schedules, standard museum hours apply.

### 2. Kumamoto was hit by a M7.1 earthquake on 2026-07-28 — five days before this trip is finalized, three months before departure
As of this writing (2026-07-29), a magnitude 7.1 earthquake struck near Uto City, ~30km southwest of Kumamoto City, causing **stone walls to collapse at 28 locations in Kumamoto Castle**, a partial collapse/gas explosion at an Aeon Mall in Kashima (with fatalities), 13+ confirmed dead, 50+ injured, ~9,000 people in evacuation centers, and **the Kyushu Shinkansen suspended across its entire route** and Kumamoto Airport disrupted. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/japan-kumamoto-earthquake-what-happened-damage-victims-latest-updates), [Mainichi](https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20260729/p2a/00m/0na/007000c), [CN Traveller](https://www.cntraveller.com/article/japan-earthquake-is-it-safe-to-travel-after-the-71-magnitude-kumamoto-quake-and-tsunami-warning), [ITIJ](https://www.itij.com/latest/news/japan-earthquake-tests-countrys-resilience-travellers-navigate-disruption). JMA warned aftershocks could continue for at least a week, with the following 2-3 days carrying the highest risk. Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka are explicitly stated as outside the impact zone with normal operations continuing.

**What this means for planning now (3 months out):** Do not cancel Kumamoto/Aso from the itinerary yet — this is exactly the kind of disruption that is typically substantially resolved within weeks for transport and within longer timeframes for heritage structures. But:
- **Re-verify Kumamoto Castle open/closed status and Kyushu Shinkansen service resumption in September/October 2026** before finalizing bookings.
- Kumamoto Castle's main keep was already fully restored as of 2026 (see below) but the wider grounds restoration wasn't due to complete until 2052 even before this quake — expect the visitable footprint to shrink further, possibly significantly, depending on damage assessment.
- Treat Kumamoto/Aso as the most schedule-fragile leg of the whole trip; consider building 1 buffer day around it or holding off final non-refundable bookings for that specific leg until ~6-8 weeks out.

## 1. KYUSHU

### Fukuoka
Gateway city; entry point is typically Fukuoka Airport (5 minutes by subway to Hakata Station) or overland from elsewhere in Kyushu. **Yatai** (open-air food stalls) cluster around Nakasu and Tenjin — classic Fukuoka evening activity, best in cooler months (late Oct/Nov weather is ideal for yatai). **Dazaifu Tenmangu** is a ~30 min rail trip from central Fukuoka (Nishitetsu line), a major Shinto shrine dedicated to the deity of scholarship, low-key half-day add-on.

### Nagasaki
- **Glover Garden + Atomic Bomb Museum/Peace Park**: standard half/full day, well-documented, no access concerns found.
- **Gunkanjima (Hashima/Battleship Island)**: mandatory guided-tour-only access (structurally unstable, solo visits prohibited) — [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/japans-local-treasures/hashima-battleship-island/). **Reliability caveat is real and material**: tours are frequently cancelled for rough seas/bad weather, and operators explicitly advise avoiding "winter, the rainy season and the typhoon season" — [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/japans-local-treasures/hashima-battleship-island/). Late October/early November sits in Nagasaki's calmer season (past typhoon peak, before winter swells), so odds are better than average, but **book with a company offering free cancellation and build in a backup day**, since some tour listings explicitly note "if the weather is bad or the waves are too high, the tour may be cancelled" ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionProductReview-g298568-d13147323-Visit_Gunkanjima_Island_Battleship_Island_in_Nagasaki-Nagasaki_Nagasaki_Prefecture.html)). A "Gunkanjima Digital Museum" exists as a fallback for weather cancellations ([kkday.com](https://www.kkday.com/en-us/product/271229)).
- **Mt Inasa night view**: officially one of Japan's "Three Great Night Views" alongside Mt Hakodate and Mt Rokko/Maya (Kobe) — [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e4412.html), [nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h00402/nagasaki-boasts-japan%E2%80%99s-top-night-view.html). Also separately certified as one of the "New Three Great Night Views of the World" alongside Hong Kong and Monaco/Shanghai — [japancheapo.com](https://japancheapo.com/travel/nagasaki-mount-inasa-view/), [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/nagasaki/mount-inasa). Ropeway access, 333m summit, clear-night priority.

### Kumamoto
**Castle restoration status (pre-earthquake baseline):** The main keep (tenshu) reopened in 2019 after the 2016 earthquakes and was reported as "restored" as of 2026, but **full restoration of the entire castle complex (walls, turrets, grounds) is not expected until 2052** — [kumamoto.guru](https://kumamoto.guru/walking-together-toward-full-restoration-by-2052%EF%BC%9Akumamoto-castle-crowdfunding/). A specific restricted area, Takenomaru, was due to open to the public only on select special days in Aug/Sept 2026 — [kumamoto.guru](https://kumamoto.guru/kumamoto-castles-hidden-takenomaru-area-opens/). **This baseline is now overtaken by the July 28, 2026 M7.1 quake** (see critical correction #2 above) — stone walls collapsed at 28 locations. **Action item: re-check status in Sept/Oct 2026.**

### Mt Aso
**Volcanic alert level, confirmed via JMA-sourced reporting:** raised to **Level 2 (Crater Area Restriction)** on **June 21, 2026** ([kumamoto.guru](https://kumamoto.guru/mt-aso-alert-raised-to-level-2-what-visitors-need-to-know/)), meaning entry within a 1km radius of Nakadake Crater is prohibited ([jocjapantravel.com](https://www.jocjapantravel.com/kyushu-kumamoto-mount-aso/)). Independent monitoring corroborates ongoing unrest: JMA noted increasing volcanic tremor amplitude from July 4, 2026, with white plumes to 300m and moderate SO2 emissions ([VolcanoDiscovery](https://www.volcanodiscovery.com/aso/news.html)); overall status is characterized as "restless (2 out of 5)" ([VolcanoDiscovery](https://www.volcanodiscovery.com/aso.html)). A section of the crater area has additionally been under emergency works closure through end of July 2026 for unrelated infrastructure reasons ([japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/aso)). **Bottom line: as of mid-2026, direct crater-rim access is closed and has been for months; this is not guaranteed to reopen by October.** Caldera-rim viewpoints, the wider Aso-Kuju National Park, and grassland/scenic driving routes remain unaffected by the crater closure — plan Aso as a caldera-scenery destination, not a guaranteed crater-viewing destination, and check the live status page ([aso-volcano.jp](http://www.aso-volcano.jp/eng/index.html)) close to departure.

### Takachiho Gorge
Boat rentals (rowboats under 17m Manai Falls) use a strict online reservation system: **bookings open exactly 2 weeks before the visit date at 9:00 AM JST**, no phone reservations — [takachiho town Tourism Association](https://en-62123.site-translation.com/guide/), [japan-kyushu-tourist.com](https://www.japan-kyushu-tourist.com/how-to-book-a-takachiho-gorge-boat-ride/). One traveler blog describes the booking window as a genuine "logistical nightmare" that frequently sells out within minutes — [japanhandbook.com](https://japanhandbook.com/takachiho-gorge-boat-rides-and-mythology-sites/). **Action item: mark calendar for exactly 14 days before your planned Takachiho date and be online at 9:00 AM JST.** Heavy rainfall causes temporary closures and can discolor the famous emerald water — [takachihogorge.com](https://takachihogorge.com/). No specific foliage-peak citation for Takachiho itself was found in this pass; treat it similarly to nearby Kyushu mountain foliage (mid-to-late November for lower valley elevations — see Weather section below), though the gorge is walkable/viewable year-round regardless of boat availability.

### Kurokawa Onsen, Beppu vs Yufuin
**Beppu vs Yufuin comparison is well-documented and consistent across sources:** Beppu is a "bustling seaside city," louder, cheaper, more urban, famous for geothermal "hells" (jigoku) and large public bathhouses; Yufuin is a "curated boutique experience" in a mountain valley — quieter, more romantic/upscale, boutique shops, art museums, private ryokan baths — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/beppu-vs-yufuin-comparison), [triptojapan.com](https://www.triptojapan.com/zh/blog/yufuin-vs-beppu). For a couple on a 2nd trip prioritizing atmosphere over budget, **Yufuin edges out** as the better fit; Beppu is worth a half-day for the jigoku circuit even if not staying overnight. Kurokawa Onsen (a third option) is characterized as the most rustic/traditional of the three, in a misty mountain valley — [jw-webmagazine.com](https://jw-webmagazine.com/tips/the-ultimate-kyushu-onsen-guide/). If only one onsen town fits the schedule, Kurokawa or Yufuin over Beppu for a couple's trip; both towns sit ~35km apart by mountain road — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/beppu-to-yufuin).

### Kagoshima + Sakurajima, Ibusuki
Kagoshima City offers direct views of active Sakurajima volcano across Kinko Bay, reachable by short ferry — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/kagoshima-itinerary-best-things-to-do-and-sakurajima-volcano-tours). **Ibusuki sand baths (sunamushi)** are a distinctive, low-effort, high-payoff activity — naturally heated volcanic sand baths, described as a "rare geothermal experience... not found anywhere else in the world" — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/kagoshima/ibusuki-sand-bath). Ibusuki is a straightforward day trip from Kagoshima via the scenic **Ibusuki Nanohana limited express** train along Kinko Bay — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/ibusuki-day-trip-from-kagoshima), [crossroadfukuoka.jp](https://www.crossroadfukuoka.jp/en/itineraries/kagoshima_plan).

### Yakushima
- **Access from Kagoshima:** three options — (1) **Toppy/Rocket jetfoil**, fastest at 2–2.5 hours, ~¥12,700 one-way/¥25,400 round-trip, but passengers stay seated indoors ([japancheapo.com](https://japancheapo.com/travel/how-to-get-to-yakushima-island/), [rezio.shop](https://bushisgo.rezio.shop/en-US/product/A-0609)); (2) car ferry ("Ferry Yakushima 2"), ~4 hours; (3) **direct flights** from Kagoshima Airport, Fukuoka, and Osaka Itami — [yakushima.kagoshima.jp](https://www.town.yakushima.kagoshima.jp/soshiki/kanko_machizukuri/kankosushin/kankosite_English/568.html). For a couple with limited days, the jetfoil is the practical default; flying saves time if arriving directly from Fukuoka.
- **Weather:** September–November is cited as typically the driest window on an island famously known for heavy year-round rainfall — "rain gear regardless of when you visit" is still standard advice — [triptojapan.com](https://www.triptojapan.com/zh/blog/yakushima-hiking-guide-best-trails-tips-and-advice).
- **Jomon Sugi hike — feasibility assessment for a couple with normal fitness:** this is a serious full-day undertaking, not a casual walk. Multiple independent sources converge: ~22km round trip via the standard Arakawa Trail, ~700m elevation change, **9–12 hours** total, requiring an early pre-dawn start to finish by sunset — [env.go.jp official park guide](https://www.env.go.jp/en/park/yakushima/ywhcc/route/3-1.html) ("at least 9 hours... requires ample physical strength, trekking experience"), [hikelist.com](https://hikelist.com/hikes/jomon-sugi-trail/reviews), [yesyakushima.com](https://yesyakushima.com/event-item/jomon-sugi-tour/) ("guests should be in good physical fitness and health"), [voyapon.com](https://voyapon.com/9-hour-hike-wilson-stump-great-jomon-sugi/) ("long and arduous"). **Verdict: feasible for a reasonably fit couple willing to commit a full day starting before dawn, but this is a genuine hiking day, not a sightseeing day — it eats an entire day of the trip and requires good weather and daylight margin.** A far shorter, easier alternative exists: **Yakusugi Land**, an 80-minute loop trail through ancient cedar forest, suitable if the couple wants Yakushima's atmosphere without the full commitment — [env.go.jp](https://www.env.go.jp/en/park/yakushima/ywhcc/route/difficulty.html).

### Takeo/Mifuneyama
No specific 2026 foliage peak-date citation was found for Mifuneyama Rakuen in this pass; treat similarly to regional Kyushu lowland/hill foliage (mid-to-late November — see Weather section). Flag as a research gap if this is a must-do stop; a dedicated Mifuneyama Rakuen foliage-illumination-event date check closer to travel is recommended (the garden is well known for a seasonal night illumination event).

### Seven Stars in Kyushu / 36+3 sightseeing trains
These are ultra-luxury sleeper/day trains, not budget sightseeing options. Seven Stars in Kyushu tickets have been reported up to **~$38,000 for a multi-day journey** and require a **lottery entry**, not a simple purchase — [msn.com](https://www.msn.com/en-ca/news/other/no-regular-ticket-available-then-he-found-a-way-onto-japan-s-most-exclusive-train/vi-AA26XQmf), [democraticunderground.com](https://du4.democraticunderground.com/12166244). **Not realistic to plan around for this trip** unless the couple has a very high discretionary budget and applies to the lottery many months in advance; mention only as a "if money were no object" curiosity.

### Kyushu Shinkansen + Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen
- **Kyushu Shinkansen**: Hakata (Fukuoka) ↔ Kagoshima-Chuo, the main north-south spine.
- **Nishi-Kyushu Shinkansen** (aka Nagasaki Shinkansen/Kamome): currently runs **only** between Takeo-Onsen and Nagasaki (66km, opened Sept 2022) — full extension to Hakata remains undecided as of 2026, so travelers from Fukuoka must transfer once at Takeo-Onsen via the "Relay Kamome" limited express — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi_Kyushu_Shinkansen), [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/how-to-get-to-nagasaki). Fastest Fukuoka(Hakata)–Nagasaki journey: ~90 minutes with one transfer, fare ~¥4,520–6,490 depending on reserved/unreserved — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/nagasaki-to-fukuoka-transport-guide), [navitime.com](https://japantravel.navitime.com/en/area/jp/guide/NTJjtg2601053-en/).
- **⚠️ Note**: this entire Kyushu Shinkansen network was suspended system-wide immediately after the July 28, 2026 earthquake — confirm normal service has resumed before finalizing rail-based itineraries.

### JR Kyushu Rail Pass 2026 pricing
Per JR Kyushu's own English site — [jrkyushu.co.jp](https://www.jrkyushu.co.jp/english/kagoshima/):
- **Northern Kyushu Area Pass**: 3-day ¥15,000 / 5-day ¥18,000
- **Southern Kyushu Area Pass**: 3-day ¥7,000
- **All Kyushu Pass** (covers the whole island, both Shinkansen and limited express): typically higher-tier; third-party aggregator [travlists.com](https://travlists.com/travel/jr-kyushu-pass/) cites roughly $127 (All Kyushu) vs $76 (North) vs $63.5 (South) as rough USD equivalents — verify exact current JPY price on jrkyushu.co.jp before purchase since pricing/tiers occasionally shift. Given a route spanning Fukuoka→Nagasaki→Kumamoto→Aso→(Kagoshima/Yakushima), **the All-Kyushu Pass is very likely worth it** if the itinerary covers both northern and southern legs by rail.

### Connecting Kyushu back to Tokyo
Fukuoka(Hakata)–Tokyo comparison — [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e4802.html), [alldayieat.com](https://www.alldayieat.com/fukuoka-to-tokyo/):
- **Shinkansen (Tokaido/Sanyo, Nozomi)**: ~5 hours, ~¥23,000, one transfer typically not required on direct Nozomi (JR Pass does not cover Nozomi without ¥8,140 supplement; Hikari/Sakura fully covered but add ~1hr + a transfer at Shin-Osaka).

## 2. SHIKOKU

### Matsuyama + Dogo Onsen
Dogo Onsen's main bath building (Honkan) underwent a multi-year renovation completed in stages through the 2020s — no fresh 2026-specific renovation-status source was surfaced in this pass beyond general listings; **verify current bathing-building access closer to travel**, as historically parts of the building rotate through partial closures for maintenance.

### Iya Valley
- **Kazurabashi vine bridge**: crossing hours vary seasonally, roughly 8:00–17:00 with summer extensions — [jeepe.jp](https://www.jeepe.jp/en/articles/iya-valley-kazura-bridge-guide-1276). Buses from Kubo to the bridge run **only on weekends and during peak seasons (April–November)**, explicitly including the "autumn color season (October to early November)" as a peak-service period — [japan-guide.com](https://s2020.japan-guide.com/e/e7829.html).
- **Foliage timing**: multiple sources converge that Iya's foliage runs **October to mid-November**, with the higher, more remote **Oku-Iya** double vine bridges area coloring earlier — "usually around the end of October to early November" — [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/de/japans-local-treasures/oku-iya-double-vine-bridges-2024/), [nishi-awa.jp](https://nishi-awa.jp/english/column/sightseeing/2522/), [tokyoweekender.com](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/location/iya-kazurabashi/). **This means late Oct/early Nov is a genuinely good foliage window for Iya** — one of the few clusters in this dossier where the trip dates align well with peak color.
- **Oboke Gorge boat ride**: standard scenic river-boat activity through the gorge; no access concerns found.

### Kotohira
Konpira-san shrine complex, famous for its long stone stairway (785 steps to the main shrine, 1,368 to the inner shrine) — standard half-day stop, no access issues found.

### Takamatsu + Ritsurin Garden
Ritsurin is one of Japan's best-preserved Edo-period strolling gardens; Takamatsu is also the main gateway port for the Setouchi islands (Naoshima/Teshima ferries depart from here as well as Uno).

### Naruto whirlpools
Best viewed when tidal current is strongest — the whirlpools are **not a fixed daily phenomenon**; visibility depends on tide state, and current speed can reach **10+ knots (~20 km/h) at spring tide**, among the fastest in Japan — [uzusio.com](https://www.uzusio.com/en/know/). **Plan the whirlpool-boat-tour or Uzu-no-Michi glass-floor-walkway visit around the tide table for your specific date**, published on the official [uzusio.com](https://www.uzusio.com/en/) site closer to travel; one operator explicitly recommends a tide-led itinerary — "if the strongest tide is in the morning, see the strait first" — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/tokushima/naruto-whirlpools). Note: one source states the single best season is **late March/early April** for whirlpool intensity ([tinytotintokyo.com](https://tinytotintokyo.com/naruto-whirlpools)), so Oct/Nov whirlpools will likely be visible but possibly not at their most dramatic — this is a tide-and-season phenomenon, not purely seasonal, so still worth doing on the correct daily tide window.

### Shimanami Kaido cycling
**Feasibility for late October: good** — autumn (Sept–Nov) is explicitly cited as one of the two optimal cycling seasons, alongside spring, for "mild temperatures, clear skies, and stunning seasonal scenery" — [triptojapan.com](https://www.triptojapan.com/blog/how-to-cycle-the-shimanami-kaido-route). 
- **Full 70km one-way (Onomichi→Imabari)**: standard e-bike rental from Onomichi Port runs ~¥2,000/day with a ¥1,100 one-way drop fee at Imabari, avoiding the need to cycle back — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/shimanami-kaido-cycling-guide-e-bike-vs-road-bike-logistics). Public rental bike systems ("Sunset Cycle"/similar) explicitly support one-way rental with return at a different island terminal — [cyclonoie.com](https://www.cyclonoie.com/en/rentalbikes-e/).
- **Time needed**: a 2-day pace is called "the sweet spot for most riders" for the full crossing with stops for citrus gelato, lunch, and sunset viewing — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/shimanami-kaido-2-day-cycling-itinerary), [touring-shimanami.com](https://touring-shimanami.com/en/2days-cycling-e/).
- **Half-route option**: a realistic single-day plan is train to Onomichi → small ferry to Mukaishima → rent bike → ride as far as **Setoda on Ikuchijima** (roughly the midpoint) → turn back or bus back — [explorehiroshima.jp](https://explorehiroshima.jp/posts/shimanami-kaido-from-hiroshima-cycling-guide). Given the couple has already visited Hiroshima (the usual gateway), **doing this from the Shikoku/Imabari side as a day or half-day ride, or the full one-way E-bike crossing with a return bus/ferry, both work.**

### Ozu/Uchiko, Cape Ashizuri, henro highlights
No dedicated deep-dive sources surfaced in this pass beyond general awareness; these are legitimate lower-priority "fair game" stops (Ozu/Uchiko historic townscapes, Cape Ashizuri as Shikoku's dramatic southern tip, a handful of the 88 henro temples as a cultural sampler) — recommend treating as flexible fillers rather than anchor destinations given transport friction (see below).

### Transport reality: does Shikoku need a car?
The island is served by JR Shikoku (Okayama is the mainland gateway via the Seto Ohashi Bridge) plus regional railways (Kotoden, Tosaden, Tosa Kuroshio, Asa Kaigan) — [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2357_shikoku.html). Rail exists between all the major cities (Matsuyama, Takamatsu, Kochi, Tokushima) but **the Iya Valley in particular is poorly served by rail** — bus access to the Kazurabashi bridge runs only on weekends/peak season per the source above, implying weekday off-peak access is thin. A rental-car guide notes it's straightforward to rent (international license or certified translation, ~¥5,000/day base) — [japanspecialist.com](https://japanspecialist.com/w/touring-shikoku-by-car). **Verdict: a car is not strictly required if the itinerary sticks to Matsuyama–Kotohira–Takamatsu–Naruto (all well-served by JR/Kotoden), but a car (or at minimum a bus day-tour) is the practical way to reach Iya Valley properly**, since public bus frequency there is limited outside peak periods, and it will double as the way to reach Oboke Gorge conveniently. Recommendation: rent a car for a 1–2 day loop through the mountainous interior (Iya + Oboke), then return it and use rail for the coastal cities.

### Shikoku itinerary shapes
- **4 days**: Takamatsu/Ritsurin (0.5) → Kotohira (0.5) → Matsuyama/Dogo Onsen (1.5) → Naruto whirlpools on the way out (0.5) → depart via Okayama. Skips Iya (requires a car detour) — only viable if the couple is not committed to Iya.
- **6 days**: Takamatsu (0.5) → Iya Valley by rental car, 2 nights (Kazurabashi + Oboke Gorge, foliage) → Matsuyama/Dogo Onsen (1.5) → Shimanami Kaido half or full day cycling toward Onomichi/back into Setouchi (1) → Naruto (0.5) → depart.

### Connecting Shikoku to Tokyo/onward
Since Shikoku has no Shinkansen of its own, the standard route is **via Okayama** (Seto Ohashi crossing by JR Shikoku limited express, then Sanyo/Tokaido Shinkansen to Tokyo) — a multi-hour combined rail journey. Alternatively, regional flights exist from **Takamatsu** or **Matsuyama** to Haneda — worth comparing cost/time against continuing overland into the Setouchi islands and flying out from Okayama or Takamatsu instead, given the itinerary's likely flow (Shikoku → Setouchi islands → mainland → onward).

## 3. SETOUCHI ART ISLANDS

*(See critical correction #1 above: no Triennale in autumn 2026 — plan as a standard, non-festival visit.)*

### Naoshima
- **Chichu Art Museum**: Tadao Ando underground museum, timed-entry/reservation system in normal operation; book ahead online.
- **Benesse House + Art House Project**: standard, well-documented, no access concerns.
- **Naoshima New Museum of Art**: **confirmed opened May 31, 2025**, the 10th Benesse Art Site Naoshima structure, designed by Tadao Ando, directed by Miki Akiko — [benesse-artsite.jp official announcement](https://benesse-artsite.jp/en/news/20250530-3251.html), [benesse-artsite.jp museum page](https://benesse-artsite.jp/en/nnmoa/). As of a Nov 2025 press update it had already surpassed 100,000 visitors — [benesse-artsite.jp](https://benesse-artsite.jp/en/news/20251121-3367.html). A further exhibition (Kei Imazu & Bagus Pandega) is scheduled for **winter 2026** — [benesse-artsite.jp](https://www.benesse-artsite.jp/en/nnmoa/exhibition/), meaning the museum will very likely have a fresh winter exhibition running by the couple's late-Oct/early-Nov visit (check exact dates near departure). **This is a genuinely new, must-see addition since a typical "1st Japan trip" itinerary — worth prioritizing.**
- **Accommodation scarcity**: Naoshima has limited lodging capacity relative to demand; book well in advance (months, not weeks) especially since this island draws international art tourists year-round independent of the Triennale cycle.

### Teshima
Teshima Art Museum (water-drop-shaped concrete shell over Rei Naito's installation) and Les Archives du Coeur (Christian Boltanski) — both standard, well-documented, no seasonal access concerns beyond the Monday closure trap below.

### Inujima
Seirensho Art Museum (built into a former copper refinery) — smallest and least-visited of the three main art islands, good for a couple wanting fewer crowds; ferry frequency here is the lowest of the group, so plan around the timetable carefully.

### Shodoshima
- **Kankakei Gorge**: ranked as one of Japan's three most beautiful gorges — [japan-guide.com](https://s2020.japan-guide.com/e/e5435.html). **Foliage peak timing is later than most of this dossier's other mountain spots**: sources converge on **the second half of November** as peak — [japan-guide.com](https://s2020.japan-guide.com/e/e5435.html), and specifically "typically peaking in late November" — [airial.travel](https://airial.travel/attractions/japan/shodoshima/kankakei-gorge-shodoshima-_DDyoUfH). One source gives an explicit foliage-season operating window of **Oct 21–Nov 30** for the ropeway — [shikoku-guide.com](http://shikoku-guide.com/en/information/kankakei/), consistent with veltra.com's Oct 21–Nov 30 ropeway schedule. **Implication: at Oct 24–Nov 11 (this trip's window), Shodoshima foliage will likely be early-to-mid stage, not full peak** — visit-worthy but manage expectations; true peak (late Nov) falls after this trip's departure date.
- **Angel Road, olive groves**: standard lower-key attractions, no access concerns.

### Ferry timetables and the Monday closure trap
This is a well-known structural risk in Setouchi trip planning: **many of the flagship museums (including on Naoshima and Teshima) close on Mondays**, and ferry frequency drops significantly outside festival periods — meaning a Monday visit can strand travelers with closed museums and thin return-ferry options. **Action item: check the specific museum and ferry Monday-closure calendars for the exact dates of the visit** (and note that when a Monday is a public holiday, closures sometimes shift to the following Tuesday) directly on benesse-artsite.jp and the relevant ferry operator sites (Naoshima/Toyo ferries, Shikoku Kisen) closer to the trip — this dossier flags the risk but exact 2026 closure calendars should be re-verified within a few weeks of travel since they are subject to periodic schedule revisions.

### Access logistics
Standard gateways are **Uno Port** (Okayama side) and **Takamatsu Port** (Shikoku side), both with regular ferries to Naoshima; Teshima and Inujima connect onward from Naoshima or directly from Uno/Takamatsu on a sparser schedule. Given this trip's routing (Shikoku → islands → onward), **Takamatsu is the natural gateway**, with a possible Uno-side exit if continuing toward Okayama/San-in.

### Days needed
**2–3 days** is realistic for a satisfying (not exhaustive) visit: Naoshima alone comfortably absorbs 1.5–2 days given the spread-out sites (Chichu, Benesse House, Art House Project, New Museum, Lee Ufan Museum); add a half or full day for Teshima and/or a shorter Inujima detour if time allows. Given the Monday trap, avoid scheduling the tightest single-day itinerary on a Monday.

## 4. SAN-IN

### Izumo Taisha
One of Japan's oldest and most important Shinto shrines (rivalling Ise in significance for some traditions); standard visit, well-documented, no access concerns found.

### Matsue Castle + Matsue city
One of only twelve castles in Japan with an original (non-reconstructed) donjon — a genuine rarity worth prioritizing over reconstructed castles seen elsewhere.

### Adachi Museum of Art garden
Ranked the **#1 garden in Japan for more than 20 consecutive years** (since 2003) by the US-based *Journal of Japanese Gardening*, and rated **3 stars (highest) by the Michelin Green Guide Japan** — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/attractions/matsue/adachi-museum-of-art), [tokyoweekender.com](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/location/adachi-museum-of-art/). Access: train from Matsue Station to **Yasugi Station** (20 min, ¥420, JR-Pass-covered), then a **free shuttle bus** every ~30 minutes (20 min ride) to the museum — [japan-guide.com](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e5806.html). **Hours**: Oct–March 9:00am–5:00pm; recommended visit duration at least 2 hours — [kankou-shimane.com](https://www.kankou-shimane.com/zh-cn/destinations/9290). Admission ¥2,500 adults (per kankou-shimane; a slightly different ¥2,300 figure appears on one aggregator — use the official prefectural figure as authoritative and reverify near travel).

### Tottori Sand Dunes
Camel rides and sandboarding are standard tourist activities here; no specific late-Oct/Nov weather concerns beyond general San-in coastal conditions (see Weather section) — good season for comfortable walking on the dunes without summer heat.

### Kurashiki Bikan district + Okayama Korakuen
Kurashiki's canal-side Edo-period merchant quarter and Okayama's Korakuen (one of Japan's "three great gardens" alongside Kenrokuen and Kairakuen) both sit conveniently between the San-in region and the Setouchi/Shikoku gateway at Okayama — natural stopover/connector points rather than standalone destinations.

### Iwami Ginzan
UNESCO World Heritage former silver mine, Omori townscape. This is a genuinely remote, low-tourist-density site — access is via limited local bus service from Oda City stations, and the main mine tunnel (Ryugenji Mabu) requires a walk/rental-bike from the townscape parking area. **This site rewards travelers willing to accept a slower, quieter day** — it is not well suited to a tight schedule without a car, given San-in's sparse rail network in this specific area.

### Amanohashidate
The famous pine-covered sandbar connecting the two sides of Miyazu Bay, with the "view from between your legs" (matanozoki) viewpoints at both ends via ropeway/chairlift — standard half-day stop, no access concerns found in this pass.

### Kinosaki Onsen
**Crab (kani) season confirmed**: multiple sources converge on **November 6 or 7** as the season opening date, running through **March 31** — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/kinosaki-winter-crab-guide) cites "6 November to late March"; **Nishimuraya Honkan** (a major Kinosaki ryokan) states explicitly "the Matsuba crab season in Kinosaki Onsen is from **November 7th** to March 31st" — [nishimuraya.ne.jp](https://www.nishimuraya.ne.jp/honkan/en/cuisine/kani/); [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/ca/news/it-s-snow-crab-season-in-kinosaki-onsen/) says simply "November to March." **The 1-day discrepancy (Nov 6 vs Nov 7) reflects the legal fishing-restriction start date vs. first-serving-in-restaurants date — treat Nov 6/7 as the practical threshold.** Given this trip departs Tokyo (i.e., leaves Japan) on **Nov 11**, if Kinosaki is visited in the last few days of the itinerary (after Nov 6/7), **the couple could catch the very start of crab season** — a genuinely well-timed opportunity if the itinerary can be arranged so Kinosaki falls after Nov 6. Note ryokan crab-course pricing runs from ¥18,000 to ¥60,000+ per person — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/kinosaki-winter-crab-guide) — and season-opening weekends will be in high demand, so book the ryokan stay early if targeting this. **Also of note**: mid-Oct to mid-Nov is when the "sea of clouds" phenomenon atop Mt Kuruhi near Kinosaki has its highest chance of occurring, typically visible around 5:30am — [visitkinosaki.com](https://visitkinosaki.com/plan/visitor-info/seasons/) — a nice bonus if staying overnight and willing to rise early.

### Tsuwano
"Little Kyoto of San-in" — a quieter, less-visited castle town with a well-preserved samurai-era streetscape; good complement to Matsue/Izumo without duplicating the Kyoto experience already had.

### Transport reality
San-in is a genuine rail desert in parts — limited-express frequency (Yakumo line to Matsue/Izumo, San-in line elsewhere) is workable for the main towns (Izumo, Matsue, Yasugi/Adachi Museum, Tottori) but thin for peripheral sites like Iwami Ginzan and Tsuwano. **A car is advantageous for a San-in loop, especially if including Iwami Ginzan**, though the core Izumo–Matsue–Adachi Museum–Tottori corridor is fully manageable by train alone.

### San-in itinerary shapes
- **4 days**: Okayama/Kyoto gateway in → Izumo Taisha (1) → Matsue + Adachi Museum (1.5) → Tottori Sand Dunes (1) → onward via Okayama Shinkansen or Yonago/Izumo flight to Tokyo (0.5).
- **6 days**: add Amanohashidate + Kinosaki Onsen (crab, if timed after Nov 6) as a northern loop before/after the Izumo–Matsue–Tottori core, plus Iwami Ginzan or Tsuwano as a quieter interior day (car recommended for this extension).

### Connecting San-in to/from Osaka/Okayama/Kyoto and onward to Tokyo
Gateway options: **Okayama** (Sanyo Shinkansen hub, then limited express into San-in) is the most efficient rail gateway; **Kyoto/Osaka** also connect via limited express (e.g., to Amanohashidate/Kinosaki via the San-in coast) without needing to backtrack through Osaka's core (already visited). For the return/onward leg to Tokyo: **flights from Yonago (Miho-Yonago) or Izumo airports** direct to Haneda are a realistic option and save substantial time versus the long rail routing back via Okayama's Shinkansen; compare cost/time against continuing overland into the Kii Peninsula or Tokyo directly, since San-in's geographic position (northern side of western Honshu) makes a flight-out from Yonago/Izumo often more efficient than the equivalent Shinkansen routing.

## 5. KII PENINSULA

### Koyasan
- **Shukubo (temple lodging)**: a well-established booking system; **Ekoin temple** (a commonly recommended shukubo) allows online reservations **up to 11 months in advance** — [ekoin.jp](https://www.ekoin.jp/en/). Morning Buddhist service + Goma fire ritual included for overnight guests without extra reservation/fee — [ekoin.jp](https://www.ekoin.jp/fr/). **Book this early** given the trip is planned ~3 months out and shukubo capacity is limited, especially around the Oct/Nov foliage season.
- **Okunoin night tour**: a guided English-language evening walk through the cemetery is a bookable commercial activity (e.g., Japan Experience's "Night-time guided tour at Okunoin," guide meets guests at accommodation ~7pm) — [japan-experience.com](https://www.japan-experience.com/activities/koyasan/okunoin-night-walk). One 2-day Koyasan itinerary guide stresses **arriving by noon** on day 1 to experience the evening rituals without feeling rushed — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/koyasan-itinerary).
- **Foliage timing**: no single definitive peak-date citation surfaced in this pass, but given Koyasan's elevation (~800m), expect **foliage to run roughly mid-to-late November**, later than lowland Kansai (already seen) but earlier than the very latest lowland spots — this is a reasonable inference from elevation/latitude patterns seen elsewhere in this dossier (e.g., Iya Valley at similar mid-mountain elevation peaking late Oct–mid Nov); **recommend rechecking a dedicated Koyasan koyo forecast closer to the trip** since this specific figure wasn't independently confirmed.

### Kumano Kodo — realistic day-walk sections for a couple
The **full Nakahechi traverse** (Takijiri-oji to Nachi Taisha) is **~70km, 5 walking days, "hard" difficulty, ~3,400m cumulative ascent** — [hikelist.com](https://hikelist.com/hikes/kumano-kodo-pilgrimage-routes) — **not realistic as a single-day activity.** For a couple wanting an authentic taste in 1 day, the most commonly recommended, genuinely "realistic" segment is:
- **Hosshinmon-oji to Kumano Hongu Taisha**: **7km, described by the official Tanabe City Kumano Tourism Bureau as "a very popular half-day walk"** with views of rural country life, tea fields, and scenic landscape — [tb-kumano.jp](https://www.tb-kumano.jp/en/kumano-kodo/nakahechi/tsugizakura-oji-to-kumano-hongu-taisha/chikatsuyu-oji-to-kumano-hongu-taisha/). This is accessed by local bus to the Hosshinmon-oji trailhead, walking down to Hongu Taisha — a well-supported, low-risk half-day hike, not requiring hard-core trekking fitness.
- A longer but still single-day-feasible option is **Takijiri-oji to Chikatsuyu** (the first of the 5 traditional walking days), which multiple sources describe as challenging but doable in a day for reasonably fit hikers — [yamakei-online.com](https://en.yamakei-online.com/yama-ya/detail.php?id=2767); this involves steeper climbing (Mt Tsurugi) and is a bigger undertaking than the Hosshinmon-oji segment.
- **Recommendation for a couple with normal (non-hiker) fitness**: do the **Hosshinmon-oji → Hongu Taisha** half-day segment as the anchor Kumano Kodo experience; this captures the atmosphere (oji shrines, forest, farmland views) at manageable effort and length.

### Yunomine Onsen
Reputed to be the **oldest onsen in Japan**; its signature experience is the **tsuboyu** — a tiny UNESCO-registered private bathhouse over the hot spring source, which operates on a public reservation/turn system (visitors sign up and wait for their turn, roughly 30-minute slots) rather than a phone/online pre-booking system — standard practice at this specific bath, confirm current reservation mechanics on arrival at the local information point since procedures can be adjusted seasonally.

### Nachi Taisha + Daimonzaka + Nachi Falls
The approach via the stone-paved **Daimonzaka** slope (moss-covered cedar-lined stairway) leading up to Nachi Taisha, with **Nachi Falls** (Japan's tallest single-drop waterfall) visible from the shrine complex — a highly photogenic, moderate-effort combined stop, well-documented, no access concerns found.

### Ise Jingu + Toba/Shima pearls
- **Ise Jingu**: standard visiting order is **Geku (outer shrine) first, then Naiku (inner shrine)** — this is the traditional/recommended sequence, though not mandatory; access to Geku is via Iseshi Station (from Osaka: Kintetsu limited express from Tsuruhashi, ~1h40, then walk; total ~2 hours from Osaka) — [japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/en/itineraries/ise-shrine-toba-ago-bay-and-beyond/), [japan.travel National Parks](https://www.japan.travel/national-parks/parks/ise-shima/how-to-get-there/).
- **Toba/Mikimoto Pearl Island**: reachable from Toba Station (5–9 min walk to the footbridge) — [kintetsu.co.jp](https://www.kintetsu.co.jp/foreign/english/sightseeing/mie/spot13.html); comfortably fits inside a 2-hour visit including the Pearl Museum and live Ama-diver demonstrations — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/mikimoto-pearl-island). Toba connects from Ise in 15–20 minutes by local train — [japanactivity.com](https://japanactivity.com/how-to-get-to-toba).

### Yoshino
Famous almost exclusively for spring cherry blossoms; **no significant autumn-specific draw was surfaced** in this pass beyond generic mountain scenery — **treat as a lower priority for an Oct/Nov trip**, not a must-include stop given the season mismatch with its primary appeal.

### Wakayama access logistics
The Kii Peninsula is genuinely rail-thin in its interior/southern reaches. The JR Kii line hugs the coast with moderate frequency, but reaching Kumano Kodo trailheads and Yunomine Onsen from the coast requires **Kumano Kotsu buses**, which run on a limited schedule — plan bus timings carefully in advance rather than assuming turn-up-and-go frequency. Koyasan itself is well served by the dedicated Nankai Koya Line + cable car from Osaka (Namba), making it the easiest single stop in this cluster to reach without a car; the deeper Kumano Kodo/Yunomine/Nachi area is where transport friction is highest.

### Kii Peninsula itinerary shapes
- **4 days**: Koyasan (1 night shukubo) → Hosshinmon-oji to Hongu Taisha half-day walk + Yunomine Onsen night (1) → Nachi Taisha/Daimonzaka + onward to Ise (0.5 travel-heavy day) → Ise Jingu + Toba pearls (1) → depart via Nagoya/Osaka.
- **6 days**: as above, plus an extra buffer day for either a second, longer Kumano Kodo segment (Takijiri-oji to Chikatsuyu) or a slower pace through the Yunomine/Nachi area, plus more time at Ise-Shima (e.g., a Toba Bay cruise) before continuing on.

### Connecting Kii Peninsula back to Kyoto/Osaka and onward to Tokyo
Standard gateway is Osaka/Kyoto for Koyasan (Nankai line) and the Kumano Kodo interior (via Kii-Tanabe), while Ise/Toba connects more naturally toward **Nagoya** (Kintetsu direct services) as the exit point. From Ise/Toba, the onward routing to Tokyo is most efficient via **Kintetsu to Nagoya, then Tokaido Shinkansen to Tokyo** (~1h40 to Nagoya + ~1h40 Shinkansen = manageable half-day connection), rather than looping back through Osaka. This also conveniently sets up the Kii Peninsula as a natural "closing loop" before the final Tokyo days, entering via Osaka/Kyoto-side (Koyasan/Kumano) and exiting via the Nagoya/Ise side toward Tokyo.

## 6. WEATHER, FOLIAGE, AND TYPHOON RISK (2026-10-24 to 2026-11-11)

### General climate pattern
Late October through mid-November across southern/western Japan (Kyushu, Shikoku, Setouchi, San-in, Kii) is generally the **most comfortable travel window of the year** for these regions: past the summer heat and humidity, past peak typhoon season, with generally clear autumn skies and daytime temperatures typically in the mid-teens to low-20s °C in most of these areas, cooling into single digits at higher elevations (Aso, Iya, Koyasan) overnight. No single authoritative city-by-city temperature table was compiled with hard numbers in this pass beyond the general pattern established across sources above — **for exact daily forecasts, consult JMA (jma.go.jp) or a foliage-forecast tracker within 1-2 weeks of each specific leg**, since medium-range forecasts this far out are not meaningfully more precise than seasonal climatology.

### Foliage (koyo) timing — key finding: southern/lower-elevation Japan peaks LATER than this trip's window in several cases
This dossier's per-region research repeatedly surfaced a consistent pattern: **higher-elevation or higher-latitude spots align well with Oct 24–Nov 11, but many lower-elevation/more southern spots peak in the second half of November, after this trip's departure date.**
- **Iya Valley (Shikoku, mid-mountain elevation)**: peaks **end of October to mid-November** ([japan.travel](https://www.japan.travel/de/japans-local-treasures/oku-iya-double-vine-bridges-2024/), [tokyoweekender.com](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/location/iya-kazurabashi/)) — **well-aligned with this trip.**
- **Shodoshima/Kankakei Gorge (Setouchi, low elevation)**: peaks **second half of November / "typically late November"** ([japan-guide.com](https://s2020.japan-guide.com/e/e5435.html), [airial.travel](https://airial.travel/attractions/japan/shodoshima/kankakei-gorge-shodoshima-_DDyoUfH)) — **this trip's Nov 11 departure will likely miss true peak**, though the ropeway's foliage-season operating window (Oct 21–Nov 30 per [shikoku-guide.com](http://shikoku-guide.com/en/information/kankakei/)) confirms color will be underway, just not at its most vivid, during the visit.
- **Koyasan (Kii, ~800m elevation)**: no hard citation found; inferred mid-to-late November based on elevation analogues elsewhere in this dossier — **verify closer to travel.**
- **Kumano/Nachi area (Kii, lower elevation, subtropical-influenced)**: no specific citation found in this pass; given its southerly, warmer, near-coastal character (similar climate zone to Kii's coast generally), **likely peaks even later than Koyasan, possibly into late November/early December** — treat any foliage there as a bonus rather than a planning anchor.
- **Kyushu mountains (Aso, Kurokawa, Takachiho)**: no hard citations were surfaced for these specific spots in this pass; by analogy to the elevation/latitude pattern above, expect **mid-to-late November** for genuine peak at Kyushu's mountain elevations, meaning this trip's window likely catches early-to-transitional color rather than peak in Kyushu specifically.
- **Yakushima**: foliage is not a major seasonal draw there in the same way (subtropical, evergreen-forest-dominated) — the island's appeal is the ancient cedar forest year-round, not autumn color.

**Overall takeaway: don't build the itinerary around "peak foliage everywhere" — Iya Valley is the standout aligned match; everywhere else, expect early-to-mid-stage color rather than the most vivid postcard peak, with Kyushu and Kii's lower-elevation spots being the furthest from peak during this specific window.**

### Typhoon tail risk
Japan's typhoon season runs roughly May/June through October, with **August and September as the clear peak months** — [japantravel.com](https://en.japantravel.com/guide/typhoons-in-japan/60540). JMA's own historical average shows typhoon *formation* rates declining sharply into autumn: **5.7 in August, 5.0 in September, 3.4 in October** — [weather-jwa.jp](https://weather-jwa.jp/en/news/articles/post6763) — and this trip's window (Oct 24–Nov 11) sits in the tail of even that October figure, extending into November when typhoon formation drops further still. Most typhoons form and make landfall **July through October** specifically because ocean temperatures are highest in that window — [nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h02096/). **Conclusion: genuine typhoon risk for this specific Oct 24–Nov 11 window is low but not zero** — a late-forming typhoon or a slow-moving system's remnants reaching Kyushu/Shikoku in this window is an occasional, not routine, occurrence. One 2026-specific seasonal outlook flagged a *possible* El Niño-linked chance of "more developed typhoons... in autumn" this year specifically — [weather-jwa.jp](https://weather-jwa.jp/en/news/articles/post13375) — worth a light mental note but not a reason to avoid this window, which remains one of the two best shoulder seasons of the year for southern Japan travel. **Practical advice: build 1 flexible/buffer day into the itinerary (ideally around the Gunkanjima boat tour and/or the Naruto whirlpool boat, the two weather-cancellable activities identified in this dossier), and monitor JMA typhoon tracking (jma.go.jp) starting ~10 days before departure.**

| Cluster | 4-day shape | 6-day shape | 8-day shape | Best connection to/from Tokyo |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Overall trip shape suggestion**: given Tokyo is reserved for the end, and Kii's Nagoya-side exit flows naturally toward Tokyo, a strong macro-sequence is: **Kyushu (fly in/around) → Shikoku → Setouchi islands → San-in (as a northern loop, possibly combined with a return toward Kyoto-adjacent territory) → Kii Peninsula (entering via Osaka/Koyasan side, exiting via Ise/Nagoya side) → Tokyo.** This minimizes backtracking, uses flights for the two long north-south jumps (Fukuoka and San-in legs) and rail for the naturally rail-friendly middle section (Shikoku–Setouchi–Kii), and lets the Kinosaki crab-season timing land near the end of the trip if the couple wants to chase that Nov 6/7 opening date.
