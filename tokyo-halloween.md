# Halloween in Tokyo — Sat 31 October 2026, arriving that morning + plus-size costume shopping

Research date: 2026-07-29. Couple: 2 adults, Madrid, European build, 2nd Japan trip, arriving Tokyo morning of Sat 31 Oct 2026 from northern Japan.

## 1. Shibuya Halloween — current reality (2024 & 2025, projecting to 2026)

**Bottom line: Shibuya is not "cancelled" — it still fills with costumed crowds — but it has been deliberately turned into a heavily policed, no-fun transit corridor, not a party.** The ward's explicit goal since 2023 has been to make it boring enough that people stop coming. It has been reasonably effective.

### Timeline of the crackdown
- **2018**: crowd flips a truck in Shibuya during Halloween chaos — triggering point for regulation. ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/tokyos-shibuya-shinjuku-wards-take-measures-to-prevent-disruptive-behavior-tourist-surge-on-halloween/))
- **2019**: Shibuya Ward enacts an ordinance prohibiting "disruptive behavior" (street drinking etc.) specifically around Halloween. ([Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/tokyos-shibuya-shinjuku-wards-take-measures-to-prevent-disruptive-behavior-tourist-surge-on-halloween/))
- **2023**: Mayor Ken Hasebe publicly says "don't come to Shibuya for Halloween" — first explicit "we don't want you here" messaging. ([Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/shibuya-halloween/))
- **June 17, 2024**: Ward Assembly unanimously **expands the drinking ban to be permanent and year-round** (not just Halloween) in key areas (6pm–5am), to curb violence/vandalism throughout the year, not only on Halloween night. ([Tokyo Weekender](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/japan-life/shibuya-halloween-2025-rules-and-restrictions/), [Travel in Japan](https://www.travelinjapan.net/shibuya-shinjuku-halloween/))
- **Oct 2024**: First Halloween under the permanent ban + intensified crowd control (barriers, temporary Hachiko statue cover, traffic controllers). ([Kyodo](https://english.kyodonews.net/news/2024/10/13c3dbbe5ae2-halloween-crowd-control-tight-in-tokyos-shibuya.html))
- **Oct 2025**: Same playbook, further hardened, with explicit messaging targeted at **foreign tourists** specifically (see below). Rain on the night reduced turnout further. ([Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/people-brave-rain-to-visit-tokyos-shibuya-on-halloween-under-crowd-control), [Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/16130934))

### What actually happened on the ground — Oct 31, 2024 (verified eyewitness account)
A SoraNews24 reporter who lives in Shibuya described the night directly ([SoraNews24, Nov 1 2024](https://soranews24.com/2024/11/01/shibuya-halloween-had-a-very-different-feel-this-year/amp/)):
- Local Burger King and other businesses **closed for the day** specifically to stop people congregating.
- At 6pm the usual Halloween crowd at the station wasn't there — but climbing up into **Hachiko Square, the mood flipped instantly**: constant loudspeaker police announcements ("Yes, it's a nice costume, but please don't stop!"), described as feeling like "an apocalyptic film."
- **Center Gai** (the main nightlife street) had ~5x the normal foot traffic, funneled through barriers into a one-way human conveyor belt — "so crowded [he] feared a crush if the line stopped."
- Riot-line police physically stood in the middle of the crowd, reprimanding anyone who paused. **No lingering, no photos, no stopping** was possible.
- Rows of police buses lined the streets. No street drinking observed — enforcement made it "near impossible."
- His own verdict: relief as a resident that the chaos is gone; but explicitly framed as bad news for anyone hoping to "party."

### 2025 rules (the version you'll likely see repeated for 2026)
- **Temporary enclosure/fencing around the Hachiko statue**, Oct 30 – Nov 1, to remove the main photo-op gathering point. ([Mainichi](http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20251003/p2a/00m/0na/029000c))
- **Year-round public drinking ban 6pm–5am** in the core zone around Shibuya Station (this is now a standing ordinance, not a one-night rule). ([Travel in Japan](https://www.travelinjapan.net/shibuya-shinjuku-halloween/))
- **Convenience stores / liquor shops in Shibuya Center Gai asked to voluntarily stop selling alcohol** around Halloween. ([Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/16130934))
- **Traffic restrictions** around the station; barrier-funneled pedestrian flow through Center Gai.
- **Explicit "don't come" messaging aimed at foreign visitors specifically** — Mayor Hasebe stated in Oct 2025 that "the majority of those coming in costumes have been foreign tourists" and reiterated Shibuya welcomes visitors "at any time — just not during Halloween or New Year's countdown." ([Japan Times, Oct 3 2025](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2025/10/03/japan/society/shibuya-halloween-restrictions/))
- No reported major incidents in Shibuya/Shinjuku either year — the crackdown is "working" by the ward's own metric. ([Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/15491125))
- Shinjuku Ward runs a parallel ordinance for the Kabukicho area (alcohol sales request + street drinking ban, 5pm Oct 31 – 5am Nov 1). ([Asahi](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/15457602))

### Is it worth going in 2026?
Realistically **no, not as a "Halloween party" destination** — go into this with the correct expectations:
- You will see costumes (mostly on tourists doing exactly what you're doing — coming to look), but you cannot stop, gather, drink, or take unhurried photos.
- The scramble crossing / Center Gai experience in 2026 will most likely be: **barrier-funneled crowd walk under heavy police supervision**, not a street party. Tokyo Cheapo's own 2026 event listing for "Shibuya Halloween" is literally titled **"(Cancelled)"** as the standing description they now use every year. ([Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/shibuya-halloween/))
- It is a legitimate 30–45 minute "see it once, take a photo of the scramble, move on" stop **if you're already in the area** — not a destination to build the whole night around, and not somewhere you can wear an elaborate costume and expect to enjoy it (impossible to stop for photos, impossible to sit down anywhere for a drink).

## 2. Alternatives, ranked, with 2026 status

### 🥇 #1 — Ikebukuro Halloween Cosplay Festival (Ikehalo)
The single best-organized, still-thriving, foreigner-friendly costume event in Tokyo.
- **2025 dates**: Oct 24–26, 2025 (Fri night event + Sat/Sun main festival) — always the **weekend before Halloween**, not on the 31st itself. ([Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival/), [Bakabuzz](https://bakabuzz.com/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival-2025-tokyo-family-fun-global-appeal))
- Held across **Sunshine City** and the surrounding Ikebukuro district, includes a **cosplay parade down Sunshine 60-dori**, stage shows/contests, and a Friday-night "Ikehalo Night."
- **Registration required to participate as a cosplayer** (lottery/抽選 for the parade slot per Time Out Japan); **free to just watch**, ticketed if you want cosplay/photo access — single-day ~¥2,980, 3-day pass ~¥10,000, night event ~¥3,480. ([Time Out Tokyo](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/things-to-do/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival), [Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival/))
- Has **dedicated changing rooms** (a real, practical advantage) — the Premium 3-Day Ticket includes early changing-room access + luggage storage. ([Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival/))
- **Caveat for your dates**: this event is the weekend *before* Halloween (2025 was 24–26 Oct), so for 2026 it will most likely fall ~Oct 23–25 — **before your arrival on Oct 31**. Confirm exact 2026 dates closer to the time via [tokyocheapo.com/events/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/ikebukuro-halloween-cosplay-festival/), but plan on this NOT being available to you.
- Still worth walking through Ikebukuro/Otome Road on the 31st itself — residual costumed foot traffic and open cosplay shops (see §3), just not the ticketed festival.

### 🥈 #2 — Kawasaki Halloween Parade (La Cittadella) — **DEAD, do not plan around it**
- Historically Japan's biggest costume parade (Italian-themed plaza "La Cittadella" in front of Kawasaki Station), usually the Sunday before Halloween.
- **Confirmed permanently closed since 2021.** Japan Travel's own event page for it states flatly: "This event closed permanently in 2021." ([Japan Travel](https://en.japantravel.com/kanagawa/halloween-parade-kawasaki/16341))
- La Cittadella's actual current autumn programming for 2025/2026 is a winter illumination event (mid-Nov–Feb) and unrelated seasonal fairs — no Halloween parade in the calendar. ([La Cittadella](https://lacittadella.co.jp/), [Matcha](https://matcha-jp.com/en/26259))
- **Do not plan a Sun 1 Nov 2026 trip to Kawasaki for this — it does not exist anymore.** (Correcting a common outdated assumption — many older blog posts still describe it as active; they are stale.)

### #3 — Roppongi / Shinjuku Ni-chome / club scene
- This is genuinely the most viable "actual party" option and is **unaffected by the Shibuya ordinance** (different ward, different vibe — indoor/bar-centric rather than street-centric).
- **Shinjuku Ni-chome (LGBTQ+ district)**: described as transforming "into a glittering carnival of drag queens, monsters, and partygoers from around the world" over Halloween weekend — open, indoor, costume-friendly, historically one of the most fun and least policed Halloween scenes in Tokyo. ([Japan Gay Guide 2025](https://japangayguide.com/travel-tips/halloween-in-japan/))
- **Roppongi**: club/bar district (A-Life, Muse, Alife, etc.) with dedicated Halloween club nights and ParaPara/Eurobeat parties; international crowd, indoor venues, no street-drinking-ban issue since it's contained in venues. ([Reformatt](https://www.reformatt.com/blog/tokyo-halloween))
- These are lower-key to research in advance (venue-specific, sold via ticket platforms like Peatix/RA closer to the date) — worth checking specific venue listings 2–4 weeks before departure.

### #4 — Tokyo Disneyland / DisneySea Halloween
- **2026 event runs Sept 16 – Oct 31, 2026** at both parks. ([Disney Tourist Blog](https://www.disneytouristblog.com/halloween-guide-tokyo-disneyland-disneysea/), [WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2026/07/tokyo-disney-resort-halloween-2026-dates-details/))
- **Costume rule for 2026**: full character costumes **are allowed Sept 15–30 and Oct 16–31**, but guests are asked to **refrain from costumes Oct 1–15**. ([Official Tokyo Disney Resort](https://www.tokyodisneyresort.jp/en/dream/event/costumes2026.html), [Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/events/tokyo-disney-resort-halloween/))
- **Good news for your date**: Oct 31 falls inside the Oct 16–31 costume-allowed window, so you CAN wear a costume into the park that day.
- **Bad news**: Halloween weekend/day at Tokyo Disney is one of the single most crowded days of the year — expect very long queue times, a packed park, high ticket demand (book weeks ahead), and higher seasonal pricing. This is a full-day, single-purpose plan (not combinable with an evening city Halloween scene) and it's a ~15–30 min train ride each way from central Tokyo (Keiyo/Maihama). Only choose this if the couple specifically wants "Disney parade + fireworks" over "street/nightlife Halloween."

### #5 — Harajuku / Omotesando / Shinjuku general street scene
- No specific organized event, but the pedestrianized shopping streets (Takeshita-dori, Omotesando) get a lot of casual costumed foot traffic and are unrestricted (no ordinance zone) — a decent low-effort "see costumes, take photos, shop" wander if you're not fussed about a specific event.

### 2026-specific notes
No 2026-dated official announcements for Shibuya/Ikebukuro were published as of this research (typical pattern: Shibuya ward publishes its restriction notice ~late Sept/early Oct each year; Ikebukuro festival dates get announced late summer). Re-check [tokyocheapo.com/events](https://tokyocheapo.com/events) and [timeout.com/tokyo](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo) in September 2026 for confirmed dates.

## 3. Large-size / plus-size costume shopping in Tokyo — the critical practical section

### The core problem
Japanese "free size" (フリーサイズ) or "L" sizing is cut for the average Japanese body and reliably runs **1–2 sizes smaller than the equivalent Western label would suggest**. A "free size" or "L" costume top in a regular Don Quijote Halloween aisle will likely not fit a larger European adult, especially through the shoulders/chest/hips. This is a real, well-documented problem, not a stereotype — see the dedicated plus-size retail sector that exists specifically because mainstream Japanese stores don't serve larger Western-proportioned bodies.

### Where to actually buy — ranked

**🥇 #1 — Sakazen (サカゼン) — best single answer for "will actually fit"**
- Japan's dedicated big-and-tall / plus-size retailer, **menswear and womenswear**, sizes go up to **6L (roughly US 4XL–5XL / EU equivalent ~3XL-4XL+)**. ([Rose+Japan](https://www.roseplusjapan.com/post/plus-size-tokyo), [Japan Living Guide](https://www.japanlivingguide.com/lifestyle/shopping/largesized-mens-clothes/))
- **Shinjuku branch has the largest range of any Sakazen location** — a short walk from Shinjuku Station East Exit. ([Wanderlog](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/865344/sakazen-shinjuku-store), [Matcha](https://matcha-jp.com/en/11884))
- Also carries **plus-size shoes up to 31cm** at the Shinjuku flagship. ([Go Connect](https://goconnect.jp/stories/plus-size-clothing-and-shoe-shops-in-japan/))
- Also has an **Ikebukuro branch** — useful for combining with the ACOS/Otome Road costume-shop crawl (§below) in one trip. ([Japan Living Guide](https://www.japanlivingguide.com/lifestyle/shopping/largesized-mens-clothes/))
- Sakazen does not specialize in Halloween costumes per se — its value is **base garments that actually fit** (hoodies, robes, cloaks, oversized shirts, jackets) which you then accessorize into a costume (see "safest garment categories" below). Duty-free/tax-free service available. ([Matcha](https://matcha-jp.com/en/11884))

**🥈 #2 — MEGA Don Quijote (Shibuya Honten, Shinjuku Kabukicho, Ikebukuro)**
- The default "Halloween costume in a box" option — every major branch has a **dedicated Halloween costume floor** every autumn (full outfits, wigs, accessories, makeup). ([Time Out Tokyo](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/shopping/the-best-halloween-costume-shops), [Tokyo Cheapo](https://tokyocheapo.com/place/mega-don-quijote-shibuya/))
- **MEGA Don Quijote Shibuya (the "Honten")** is one of the largest branches (7–8 floors, 5 min from Shibuya Station) and has one of the biggest costume sections in the chain. ([LiveJapan](https://livejapan.com/en/in-tokyo/in-pref-tokyo/in-shibuya/article-a0005799/))
- **Reality check on sizing and stock on the day itself**: these costumes are cut to Japanese "free size" standards. On Halloween day itself (a Saturday this year — worse), **expect the costume floor to be heavily picked over by mid-morning**, with the larger end of "free size" gone first. This is the least reliable option for a genuinely large-build European adult, and the least reliable ON THE DAY given a Saturday Halloween. Best used as a **backup/accessories run** (wigs, masks, face paint, props, cheap capes) rather than the primary plan for garments that need to fit the body.
- 10% tax-free + up to 7% extra discount coupons are commonly available for tourists (LiveJapan coupon page). ([LiveJapan](https://livejapan.com/en/in-tokyo/in-pref-tokyo/in-shibuya/article-a0005799/))

**#3 — ACOS (Ikebukuro) and the wider Otome Road cosplay-shop cluster**
- ACOS is described as "the first gate for cosplay beginners" — sells complete costumes plus character-themed casualwear "in a variety of sizes," plus wigs, shoes, and props. ([Time Out Tokyo](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/shopping/acos))
- Located in Ikebukuro alongside Cospa/Cospatio, K-BOOKS Cosplay-kan (~2,000 cosplay products: wigs, costumes, accessories), Mandarake, and other Otome Road shops. ([Tumblr field report](https://otomeblem.tumblr.com/post/120431905569/a-short-guide-to-buying-cosplay-items-in-tokyo), [LiveJapan](https://livejapan.com/en/in-tokyo/in-pref-tokyo/in-ikebukuro/article-a0000149/))
- Cosplay-specific costumes (character replicas) are often **made-to-order or sized in wider ranges than generic "party costumes"** because the cosplay industry already serves larger male and female cosplayers — worth a look even though it's more "anime cosplay" than "generic Halloween costume."

**#4 — Punyus (Shibuya 109) and Taberunosky / Re-J & Supure (Ikebukuro Sunshine City)**
- Not costume shops, but genuine plus-size **Japanese streetwear brands** that could supply a fitting base layer or an easy "costume via styling" (e.g., witch, cat, devil look built from plus-size black separates + horns/ears/accessories from a costume shop).
- **Punyus**: S–4L (~up to US 2XL). ([Rose+Japan itinerary](https://www.roseplusjapan.com/post/tokyo-plus-size-fashion-itinerary))
- **Taberunosky** (Higashi-Ikebukuro): up to 6L (~US 4XL–5XL). ([Rose+Japan](https://www.roseplusjapan.com/post/tokyo-plus-size-fashion-itinerary))
- **Re-J & Supure / Ludic Park** inside Sunshine City: up to 5L (~US 4XL). ([Rose+Japan](https://www.roseplusjapan.com/post/tokyo-plus-size-fashion-itinerary))
- These stores are aimed at Japanese plus-size women; menswear equivalent coverage is thinner — Sakazen remains the strongest option for the male half of the couple.

**General/mainstream chains (Tokyu Hands / Hands, Loft, Village Vanguard, Kiddy Land)**
- These sell **accessories, props, makeup, small novelty items** well (masks, fangs, ears, fake blood, glow items, small hats) — genuinely useful supplementary stops, but **do not rely on them for garments that need to fit a larger body**. Their apparel-adjacent stock (if any) is Japanese-sized.
- **Yodobashi**: primarily electronics; not a costume source, skip for this purpose.

### Sizing reality — how "free size" maps to EU, and safest costume categories
- Japanese **"F" (free size)** ≈ EU 38–40 women's / EU 46–48 men's at best — this is the ceiling for most mainstream retail costumes, well below what many European adults need.
- Japanese **L** in mainstream fashion ≈ EU 42–44 (women) / EU 50 (men) — still often too small.
- Dedicated plus-size stores (Sakazen, Taberunosky, Re-J) label in **3L–10L**, and Rose+Japan's own conversion states **3L–6L in physical stores ≈ US XL–5XL**, i.e., roughly **EU 44–56+** depending on brand — this is the range that will actually work. ([Rose+Japan](https://www.roseplusjapan.com/post/tokyo-plus-size-fashion-itinerary))
- **Safest garment categories for a larger body, regardless of exact size labeling** (all forgiving of fit variance):
  - **Capes, cloaks, ponchos, robes** — one-size-fits-most by design (witch, wizard, vampire, Grim Reaper, druid).
  - **Kigurumi / onesies** — Japan makes these in genuinely large adult sizes (they're a mainstream item, not niche) — check Don Quijote's kigurumi section specifically, separate from the "costume" section, sizing tends to run bigger.
  - **Inflatable costumes** — sized by height range, not body width; generally the single most size-inclusive category in any store.
  - **Masks, full-head pieces, hats, wigs, face paint, prop weapons/accessories** — zero fit risk, buy freely at Don Quijote / Tokyu Hands / Loft.
  - **Avoid**: fitted bodysuits, corseted outfits, anything advertised as "sexy [X]" cut (these run smallest of all), character costumes with tailored jackets/vests.

### Concrete shopping route for the morning/afternoon of Sat 31 Oct 2026
Given they land in Tokyo that morning, here is a realistic half-day route assuming a mid-morning arrival at a central hotel:

1. **First stop — Sakazen Shinjuku** (largest branch, near Shinjuku Station East Exit). Get properly fitting base garments — hoodies, robes, oversized jackets, plus-size shoes if needed. This is the errand most likely to fail if left for later in the day, so do it first while stock is untouched. Nearest station: **JR Shinjuku Station (East Exit)**. Typical department-store-style hours ~10:00/10:30–20:00/20:30 (confirm exact hours the week of travel via [sakazen.co.jp](https://www.sakazen.co.jp) store locator).
2. **Second stop — MEGA Don Quijote Shinjuku (Kabukicho branch)**, a short walk from Sakazen. Buy accessories, props, masks, makeup, and — if very lucky — a kigurumi/inflatable in a large size to serve as the actual "costume" layer over/with the Sakazen base garment. Open very late (many Don Quijote branches run near-24h), so this can also be a fallback in the evening if something is missing. Nearest station: **Shinjuku / Seibu-Shinjuku**.
3. **Optional third stop — Ikebukuro (ACOS + Otome Road + Sakazen Ikebukuro + Taberunosky/Sunshine City)** if time allows and they want cosplay-grade items or specifically want the anime/character route. This is a bigger detour (~15 min train from Shinjuku) — only worth it if Shinjuku's haul is insufficient. Nearest station: **Ikebukuro (JR/Metro)**.
4. **Return to hotel to change** (see §4 — do not plan to change in public).

### Should they buy in Spain instead? — clear recommendation
**Yes — bring the core costume pieces from Spain, and use Tokyo only for finishing touches and accessories.** Reasoning:
- European costume retailers (Halloween-specific chains, Amazon.es, or even a costume-rental shop in Madrid) will have **actual EU large/XL/XXL sizing** as standard stock, not as a specialty afterthought — zero fitting risk, far more style choice, and no time pressure.
- They are landing **the same morning** they need the costume — a same-day, jet-lagged, luggage-in-tow shopping trip in an unfamiliar sizing system is a real risk of arriving at the event with nothing that fits, especially since this is a **Saturday Halloween** (peak stock depletion at Don Quijote).
- The *only* genuine reason to buy everything in Tokyo would be if they specifically want a Japan-exclusive aesthetic (a very Japanese character costume, a kigurumi they can't get at home) — which is exactly the kind of item Sakazen/ACOS/Don Quijote is good for as a **supplement**, not the whole outfit.
- **Recommended split**: base costume (garment that must fit) packed from Spain; Tokyo trip used for wigs, masks, makeup, props, a Japan-specific novelty piece (kigurumi/cape), and a backup plan at Don Quijote if luggage space was limited.

## 4. Practicalities

### Where to change into costume
Japan has essentially no public street-changing culture and no expectation of it (unlike some Western Halloween scenes) — plan one of these:
- **Hotel room** — simplest option if their hotel check-in time allows a return trip mid-afternoon before heading out for the evening.
- **Coin lockers** (available at every major station — Shinjuku, Ikebukuro, Shibuya) — useful for stashing daytime clothes/luggage after changing elsewhere, not for changing itself (no changing room attached).
- **Karaoke box** — a genuinely common informal solution among locals/foreign residents: rent a private karaoke room for an hour, which comes with a mirror, space, and privacy, then walk out costumed. Cheap (~¥300-500/person/hour off-peak) and normalized.
- **Event-provided changing rooms** — Ikebukuro Halloween Cosplay Fest provides these, but per §2 the festival itself likely won't align with your Oct 31 date in 2026.
- **Department store / large shopping mall restroom** — workable in a pinch for smaller costume changes, not ideal for elaborate outfits.

### Etiquette and what draws police attention
- **No full-face masks in some venues/areas** — some bars, clubs, and train operators have explicit no-full-face-covering policies (identity/security concerns); carry face paint or a half-mask as backup if a venue looks strict.
- **No weapons or prop weapons that look real** — anything resembling a real blade, gun, or similar will draw immediate police attention; use clearly toy-styled/oversized/brightly colored props only.
- **No street drinking in Shibuya, ever, 6pm–5am (year-round ordinance, not just Halloween)** — carrying an open can/cup through that zone risks a police stop even outside Halloween. ([Travel in Japan](https://www.travelinjapan.net/shibuya-shinjuku-halloween/))
- **Do not block foot traffic, stop for photos in the middle of the scramble crossing, or linger in Center Gai** — this is the single most enforced rule in the current Shibuya regime; officers will move you along.
- General Japanese etiquette still applies: no littering (visible enforcement focus this year given past trash complaints), don't climb on structures/statues, keep noise reasonable off the main strip.

### Last trains and crowding
- On Halloween night, several train operators historically add extra late-night services to handle the surge (same pattern documented for New Year's Eve — Halloween sees a similar, if smaller, response). ([Time Out Tokyo, re: NYE service](https://www.timeout.com/tokyo/things-to-do/tokyo-new-years-eve-traffic-information))
- Expect the **Yamanote Line and any train serving Shibuya/Shinjuku/Ikebukuro to be significantly more crowded than normal from ~9pm onward**, peaking around last-train time (typically ~12:30–1am depending on line/station).
- The old "Yamanote Halloween Train" (spontaneous costumed party inside train cars) is a well-documented historical phenomenon from the 1990s–2000s but has been actively suppressed for years now (police signage, station announcements) — **do not expect or attempt to replicate this; it will draw immediate police/station-staff intervention.** ([CN Traveler](https://www.cntraveler.com/story/tokyos-halloween-subway-parties-from-the-90s-were-mysterious-boozy-and-borderline-illegal), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/japans-infamous-halloween-trains-1819762246))
- Practical tip: plan to leave whichever venue/area by ~11:30pm–midnight to have a comfortable ride back, or budget for a taxi if staying later (surge pricing likely).

## 5. Recommended single best plan for 31 Oct 2026

**Given they arrive that morning and want a genuinely fun night, not a police-managed crowd experience:**

**Morning–early afternoon**: Check into hotel, drop bags. Shopping run: **Sakazen Shinjuku** (base costume pieces that fit) → **Don Quijote Shinjuku/Kabukicho** (accessories, props, makeup, backup kigurumi) → back to hotel to change. Skip Shibuya's Don Quijote for this leg — Shinjuku Kabukicho branch is equally large and keeps them in one neighborhood.

**Early evening (~5–6pm)**: Optional 30–45 minute detour through **Shibuya scramble crossing / Hachiko area** purely as a sightseeing curiosity — go early before the heaviest police funneling sets in later at night, take a few photos, do NOT plan to eat, drink, or linger there. Treat it as "we saw the famous crossing on Halloween," not as the night's main event.

**Evening–night (main event)**: Head to **Shinjuku Ni-chome** for the LGBTQ+-district Halloween carnival atmosphere, or **Roppongi** for club/bar Halloween nights — both are indoor-venue-centric, unaffected by the Shibuya street ordinance, internationally welcoming, and genuinely festive rather than police-managed. Ni-chome in particular has a reputation as one of the most purely fun, low-hassle Halloween scenes left in Tokyo. Pick specific venues 2–3 weeks out once 2026 event listings appear on Peatix/Time Out Tokyo.

**Late night**: Head back toward the hotel before ~midnight to beat the worst of the last-train crush; if staying later, budget for a taxi.

This plan gets them: a fitting costume (low risk of failure), the "I saw Shibuya on Halloween" experience without wasting the night on it, and an actual party at the end — the best realistic outcome given 2024–2025's clearly established Shibuya reality.
